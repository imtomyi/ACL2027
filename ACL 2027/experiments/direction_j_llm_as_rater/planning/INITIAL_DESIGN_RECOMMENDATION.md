# Initial design recommendation (pre-pilot)

## Decision for the next stage

Use **12 controlled items per failure family** for the approved Dreaddit pilot.
With five failure families and one matched natural item per controlled item,
this is 60 controlled items, 60 natural items, and 120 total items. At three
ratings from each of the three evaluator roles per item, it requires 1,080
independent ratings.

Under the provisional median-time assumptions, the pilot requires about 18
researcher hours and 60 expert hours (78 human review hours total), before
training, adjudication, administration, privacy review, attrition, and repair
assessment. This is a planning estimate, not a recruitment commitment.

## Why this is a pilot rather than the final confirmatory design

The conservative scenario simulation estimates only 3.9% power at this size to
detect a 15-percentage-point aligned-expert gain after item-level majority
aggregation. It is intentionally not presented as a hypothesis test. Its job is
to estimate detection prevalence, item and rater heterogeneity, review time,
cannot-judge rates, exclusions, and the paired discordance that controls the
confirmatory sample requirement.

Using the current assumptions, the first evaluated design above 80% power has
120 controlled items per family: 600 controlled items, 600 matched natural
items, 1,200 total items, and 10,800 ratings. That implies roughly 780 human
review hours and is likely too expensive without design refinement.

## Required post-pilot decision

Rerun the simulation using the approved pilot estimates and compare:

1. the preregistered individual-rating mixed model;
2. item-level majority aggregation as a conservative sensitivity analysis;
3. a reduced natural-item ratio if natural outputs are not required one-to-one;
4. failure-family-specific minimum effects and multiplicity control; and
5. the noninferiority margin and expert-minute budget for routing.

The final design is frozen only after that comparison. No result-table cell
should be populated from these simulated values.
