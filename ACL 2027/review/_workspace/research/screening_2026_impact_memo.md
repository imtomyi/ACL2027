## Material changes to RQ1–RQ8

| RQ | Report-level change required from the inspected 2026 evidence |
|---|---|
| **RQ1—tasks and methods** | The task map now has direct 2026 examples of bottom-up taxonomy construction; open, axial, and selective coding; fixed-code assignment; inductive/deductive hybrid coding; codebook induction, reduction, and drift monitoring; hierarchy and theme construction; quotation/evidence retrieval; source-linked report generation; noncoding human-led interpretation; reflexivity support; LLM judging; and confidence-based triage. ATA-LLM and the axial-coding paper demonstrate code-to-cluster/category pipelines, not grounded-theory theory generation. Teleoscope is an adjacent large-corpus curation system rather than an LLM generator. NITA is already represented by its inspected QRP VOR in the 2025 companion and must be counted once as a counterexample to the assumption that every AI-assisted thematic workflow must begin with coding. |
| **RQ2—methodological grounding** | Claimed and operationalized methods must remain separate. Reflexis is comparatively congruent with reflexive thematic analysis because disagreement, positionality, code evolution, and analyst authority are explicit. By contrast, many papers calling their operation thematic analysis perform fixed-label classification, sentence-level code generation, embedding clustering, topic modeling, or comparison with one reference. FC01 explicitly invokes reflexive thematic analysis for an automated repeatable workflow, even though a model cannot supply situated reflexivity; FC11 maps four flat clusters to a hierarchical human codebook; FC15 is nine-class educational dialogue coding, not interpretive theme development. These operations should not be pooled as one TA-performance construct. |
| **RQ3—architecture** | The inspected papers add corpus-wide iterative prompting versus progressive batches, local Gemma/Llama workflows, staged open-code–embedding–manifold–HDBSCAN pipelines, multi-agent role orchestration, retrieval-augmented source grounding, graph-based branching/provenance, self-critique, confidence anchoring, and activation steering. Architecture labels still overstate independence: role-prompted instances of one model are not triangulating analysts. Exact open-code models, proprietary snapshots, seeds, order, and repeated runs remain frequently absent. Reflexis and Teleoscope materially narrow implemented interface/provenance novelty. Social Verbatim separately proposes open models, prompt traceability, human supervision, privacy, and data-proximate interaction, but explicitly leaves LLM integration to a future phase; QuaRUM and CATALYST remain potentially important but unretrieved. |
| **RQ4—human–AI relationship** | Human roles span post-hoc fit checking, codebook/example provision, acceptance and correction, expert validation, pair deliberation, and primary interpretive authority. Reflexis makes AI prompts optional and preserves analyst disagreement rather than forcing consensus. Teleoscope lets analysts branch and compare schemas. Misra's original coders judge model outputs against their own prior themes, which is useful domain expertise but not independent validation. The report should describe intervention points and decision authority, not repeat “human-in-the-loop” labels. |
| **RQ5—evaluation** | The new evidence strengthens the conclusion that agreement, coherence, self-confidence, and an LLM judge are not qualitative validity. FC01's iterative theme-set Jaccard scores exceed progressive batching while both miss low-frequency or latent concerns. Misra's generated codes often lack context or theme fit. FC06 finds that LLM-as-judge scores recover coarse model ordering but only moderate alignment with human interpretive ratings. FC13 shows that coherent topic models omit implicit RTA themes. FC15 shows extreme verbal-confidence miscalibration and anchoring. Evaluation must therefore include source sufficiency, omission and minority-theme recall, expert warrants, calibration/abstention curves, and stability—not only overlap, embedding similarity, cluster coherence, or model confidence. |
| **RQ6—validity threats** | Directly observed threats include prompt development and evaluation on the same corpus; short-context segmentation; rare/latent-theme loss; interviewer–diagnosis confounding; topic labels influenced by a previously seen RTA; author/developer evaluation; opaque proprietary judge rubrics; unsupported interpretation from very small chunks; confidence mode collapse; and internal numerical inconsistency. FC13's topic model under-represents or misses masking, self-doubt/loathing/embarrassment, and leveraging strengths. FC01 misses lower-frequency fraud/safeguarding, risk aversion, KYC, unique-selling-proposition, and switching-barrier themes. These failures support controlled source, participant, minority, and negative-case audits. |
| **RQ7—ethics** | The 2026 set adds local open-weight processing as a concrete privacy response, along with systems emphasizing provenance, analyst accountability, positionality, and source governance. However, consent for model processing, provider retention/training terms, embedding sensitivity, stakeholder authority, and downstream accountability remain inconsistently reported. Mutanana and Shoko offer useful conceptual safeguards—reflexivity, audit trails, participant involvement, privacy, and human oversight—but their narrative review has no reported search or synthesis method and should remain adjacent rather than empirical evidence. |
| **RQ8—opportunity** | The three ranked directions survive, but all novelty claims need narrowing. VoiceTrace cannot be sold as “adding provenance”; WarrantBench cannot be sold as “showing agreement is not quality”; StopTA cannot be sold as “stopping when no new themes appear.” The strongest open contributions are independent validation of participant/source coverage and omission, multi-expert theme-level warrant evaluation across analytic contracts, and held-out stopping tied to supported minority/negative-case gain, stability, unsupported claims, and verification burden. Confidence calibration/abstention is a strong failure axis that can be incorporated into WarrantBench rather than treated as a standalone generic confidence paper. |

## High-value quantitative findings

These figures are suitable for report synthesis if their scope and denominators are retained.

1. **Iterative versus progressive batching (FC-01).** Against a reconciled human analysis of 150 net-promoter-score comments, iterative theme-set Jaccard was .78/.86/.71 for promoter/passive/detractor comments, compared with .44/.71/.64 for progressive batching. The stronger overlap did not prevent omission or flattening of several lower-frequency concerns.
2. **Context and thematic fit of local models (FC-05).** Context/duplication/theme-fit percentages were Gemma2 inductive 38.9/28.5/19.2, Gemma2 deductive 45.6/29.5/27.1, Llama3.1 inductive 22.9/16.3/11.7, and Llama3.1 deductive 43.7/36.2/26.8. The paper's narrative reports 46.8% for the last theme-fit value, contradicting Table 2's 26.8%; the extraction preserves the table value and flags the conflict.
3. **LLM-as-judge validity (FC-06).** The best automated coherence correlations were rho=.63 with human accuracy and .67 with nuance/composite. Individual-rater ICCs were .492–.545; the averaged accuracy ICC was .782. Only 60 of 3,560 outputs received human ratings, and model-level correlations use five aggregate points.
4. **Manifold clustering (FC-11).** ATA-LLM generated 4,536 codes. UMAP and DenseMAP each reached topic coherence .893; t-SNE reached diversity .975; DenseMAP had the best silhouette, .152. Its best human-codebook embedding similarity was .77, but no independent human judged source grounding, coverage, or theme usefulness, and the open-code generator is unidentified.
5. **Reflexive deliberation (FC-03).** Twelve experienced researchers worked in six pairs. The two author-raters judged all seven naturally triggered drift alerts and all 18 positionality-aware prompts contextually relevant; 11/12 participants valued Discussion Focus and 12/12 reported retained control. There was no baseline, corpus data were synthetic, sessions were short, and evaluation was not independent.
6. **Confidence calibration (FC-15).** GPT/Gemini/Llama accuracy was .441/.520/.338 while mean verbal confidence was .908/.922/.891; ECE was .467/.403/.553 and AUC .689/.669/.565. A .95 confidence anchor was adopted in 96.5% of outputs and collapsed AUC to .518; a .05 anchor was never adopted. Held-out activation steering improved AUC only to .588/.584, still below .60.
7. **Human RTA versus topic modeling (FC-13).** The study reduced 17,953 response documents to 13,957 and selected ten topics. Topic modeling partly overlapped human themes but under-represented or missed masking/hiding, self-doubt/loathing/embarrassment, and using one's strengths; apparent diagnosis clustering was confounded by interviewer identity.

## Gap-claim corrections

1. Replace “provenance systems are absent” with “structured lineage, branchable analytic histories, source links, drift alerts, and comparison workspaces exist, but exactness, sufficiency, representativeness, omission detection, participant spread, and independent-auditor usability are not jointly validated.” Reflexis and Teleoscope materially reinforce the earlier provenance systems.
2. Replace “reflexive human–AI deliberation has not been implemented” with “Reflexis implements optional positionality prompts, drift alerts, disagreement comparison, and immutable code history; prospective, independent, longitudinal evidence that this improves interpretation remains absent.” A new project needs outcome validation, not merely another ledger/interface.
3. Replace “automatic evaluation lacks human calibration” with “some LLM-judge metrics are human-calibrated on small samples, but they are only moderately aligned and do not replace qualitative warrants.” FC06 is a direct limitation case.
4. Replace “model confidence can guide efficient review” with “verbal confidence is badly miscalibrated and can be driven into anchor-following mode collapse.” Any triage claim needs held-out calibration, repeated splits, per-code reporting, abstention/coverage curves, and independent human labels.
5. Replace “topic coherence or embedding similarity validates themes” with “coherence/similarity can organize a corpus but can miss implicit and minority meanings.” FC11's .893 coherence and .77 codebook similarity lack human source-level validation; FC13 directly shows absent human RTA themes.
6. Treat Social Verbatim's open-model, prompt-traceability, privacy, feedback, and human-authority design as a proposal, not an implemented provenance result. Its review method is unreported and its 15-user formative study concerns the earlier non-LLM beta.
7. Preserve the open participant/minority/negative-case audit claim, but scope it as reviewer judgment among inspected full texts. No inspected 2026 paper jointly measures participant-level evidence recall/precision, concentration, source shortcuts, contradiction survival, unsupported synthesis, and warrant quality under controlled perturbation.

## Impact on the three ranked ARR projects

1. **VoiceTrace remains Rank 1, narrowed rather than closed.** Reflexis supplies code histories, drift alerts, positionality prompts, and disagreement-focused deliberation; Teleoscope supplies branchable graph provenance and analyst-controlled discovery; several other systems attach sources to codes/themes. These occupy “build a provenance interface” novelty. The defensible advance is a source- and participant-aware validity audit: exact evidence precision/recall, participant coverage/concentration, rare/negative-case survival, source/identity shortcut probes, counterfactual perturbations, and blinded domain-plus-method expert judgments. FC01's omitted low-frequency concerns and FC13's omitted implicit themes strengthen the need.
2. **WarrantBench remains Rank 2, with a higher construct-validation bar.** Agreement Is Not Quality already supplies source-symmetric blind comparison; FC06 adds limited LLM-judge calibration; FC05 and FC01 expose context/fit and rare-theme failures; FC13 compares human RTA and machine topics. The open advance is theme-level, source-grounded, multi-expert, plurality-preserving evaluation crossed across analytic contracts/domains and validated on held-out models/domains. A pairwise preference study, generic “agreement is not quality” argument, embedding metric, or same-family judge is insufficient.
3. **StopTA remains Rank 3, narrowed rather than closed.** Iterative-versus-batch processing and analyst confidence in saturation now have direct examples, while the broader review already contains fixed-round, no-new-theme, drift, and refinement-trajectory systems. Teleoscope's self-reported saturation confidence is not demonstrated coverage. The remaining contribution is a frozen, held-out rule balancing marginal supported participant/negative-case coverage against redundancy, unsupported claims, instability, and human verification burden, with transfer across systems and domains.

The ranking need not change. Confidence/abstention should be a measured component of WarrantBench and perhaps VoiceTrace triage, not a fourth generic project. Reflexis also creates a true novelty conflict for any proposal whose principal contribution is merely AI-supported reflexive discussion; the surviving claim is that a particular deliberation design improves independently judged outcomes over time.
