# Systematic scoping review of LLM and NLP methods for qualitative analysis

**Search cutoff:** 24 August 2026  
**Target:** ACL Rolling Review, 12 October 2026 cycle  
**Review design:** systematic scoping review with critical methodological synthesis  
**Reporting convention:** *Author claim* reports what a source says. *Reviewer interpretation* is this review's analysis of whether the operation and evidence support that claim. Missing details are recorded as **not reported**; uncertain publication status is recorded as **not verified**.

# Executive synthesis

This review maps 237 unique, full-text-grounded evidence records: 181 core reports with one 12-dimension quality assessment each and 56 adjacent, contextual, or methodological records retained to interpret validity, ethics, and qualitative-method claims. The field has moved rapidly from asking a chat model to suggest codes to constructing hierarchies, applying codebooks at scale, orchestrating role-prompted pipelines, learning persistent instructions from analyst feedback, supporting noncoding theme development, and retaining versioned source lineage. Dai, Xiong, and Ku made codebook revision, rationale, and stopping human decisions ([Dai et al., 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.669/)); LLooM organized concept induction as an analyst-editable loop ([Lam et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642830)); HICode and Thematic-LM scaled hierarchical or role-prompted induction ([Zhong et al., 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1580/); [Qiao et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3696410.3714595)). By the cutoff, NITA kept positionality, counterevidence, theme retention, and the final meta-narrative with human researchers without making coding mandatory; Reflexis logged code evolution and surfaced drift, disagreement, and positionality prompts; and ROCK specified machine-readable source spans, codebooks, relations, and versions ([Nguyen, Trung, and Nguyen, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1080/14780887.2026.2638348); [Ye et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791275); [Peters et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/4q8bs_v2)).

What is technically possible remains narrower than “automated thematic analysis.” Models can propose manifest codes, apply defined deductive labels, cluster and name concepts, retrieve candidate quotations, draft themes and reports, compare runs, and expose intermediate artifacts for correction. They do not thereby supply researcher positionality, theoretical sensitivity, cultural accountability, or an independent warrant for interpretation. Even apparently strong code application is conditional. Muse reports κ=.703 for an ensemble on 12 well-specified codes after per-code threshold tuning, whereas Hill et al. report high percentage agreement but κ=.34 in a low-prevalence health task ([Matveyenko et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-industry.131/); [Hill et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pdig.0001189)). Dörfel and Ammoneit found 13/14 semantic code mappings above .60 and mean run Jaccard .83, yet coding-unit overlap with the human analysis was .39 and domain-specific distinctions were lost ([Dörfel and Ammoneit, 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci16081314)).

The principal weakness is construct validity, not a shortage of metrics. Parkington et al.'s two LLM workflows each missed 190/428 human excerpts; their out-of-box and knowledge-base conditions overlapped only 238/428 and 124/428 excerpts, and 68/251 purported knowledge-base quotations were interpretive summaries rather than quotations ([Parkington et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08002)). QualiGPT's GPT self-agreement (κ=.87) exceeded its human-inclusive agreement (about .46), despite human–human κ=.73 ([Zhang et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.54941/ahfe1006232)). IDEAlign's best automated correlations with expert judgments were .618/.683 versus a human oracle's .906/.812; style-only edits inflated embedding similarity by as much as 82% ([Nam et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02855)). ICR found human reliability of .86–1.00 and LLM reliability of .35–.76 even when cosine and F1 were high ([Pérez et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04413)). These results do not invalidate similarity or reliability measures; they show why each operation must be validated against its intended qualitative construct.

No-gold evaluation, provenance, reflexivity interfaces, multi-agent consensus, and elementary stopping rules are no longer empty spaces. Aggregate Code Space diagnostics preserve multiple code sets but can reward code flooding and cannot recover concepts omitted by every coder ([Chen et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.2073/)). Blind source-symmetric verification shows that lower human–LLM agreement need not imply lower expert preference, but it uses one verifier, one codebook, and code assignment rather than theme interpretation ([Liu et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28890)). Reflexis and Teleoscope implement histories, branching, and deliberation; clinical and production pipelines maintain quote or turn identifiers; CAILA, saturation-ratio studies, LOGOS trajectories, and iterative-versus-batch comparisons supply stopping-related signals ([Ye et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791275); [Lee et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791310); [Lin et al., 2025](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3995/LLMQUAL_paper4.pdf); [De Paoli and Mathis, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04859); [Pi et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24294)). **Reviewer judgment among the inspected full texts:** what remains open is independent, cross-domain validation of exactness, omission, participant spread, rare and negative cases, disagreement, and auditor usability.

Culturally and clinically situated interpretation reveals bidirectional failure. Japanese clinical work recovered common descriptive themes more readily than low-frequency or culturally specific ones; “fate” was absent ([Sakaguchi et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.2196/71521)). Open models analyzing interviews with Black male firearm-violence survivors produced thousands of labels, averaged about 10% relevance, refused an average 44% of prompts, and erased trauma, sexual-health, racial, and African American English content even when they recovered formal human codes before aggregation ([Zhu et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.591/)). Conversely, LLM-TA overemphasized a rare dramatic event ([Raza et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01620)). Subbiah et al.'s three-model, five-seed analysis of 154 longitudinal interviews found demographic-conditioned semantic, affective, and thematic shifts; experts reported stereotyping and omission of deaths, violence, abuse, and addiction ([Subbiah et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20131)). Lukito et al. further showed that topic models missed masking, self-doubt, and strengths and that apparent diagnosis clustering was confounded by interviewer identity ([Lukito et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-34570-7)). Minority preservation is therefore not a one-directional recall problem: omission, dilution, misattribution, stereotyping, refusal, and sensational overrepresentation all require measurement.

**Reviewer judgment among the inspected full texts:** the strongest October opportunity remains a plurality- and provenance-aware audit of whose voice survives aggregation, but its novelty is the joint validation, not a new ledger or interface. It should retain exact speaker-to-quotation-to-code-to-theme paths; cross participant, interviewer, site, source, and identity perturbations; quantify participant concentration and negative-case survival; and use independent qualitative and domain experts to judge evidence warrants and omissions. VoiceTrace remains Rank 1, WarrantBench Rank 2, and StopTA Rank 3, conditional on immediate access to authorized data and evaluators.

# 1. Introduction and motivation

Qualitative analysis turns situated language into claims about experience, practice, meaning, and social structure. Its labor is not merely labeling: analysts read and reread a corpus, memo, compare cases, revise concepts, inspect contradiction, locate counterevidence, and write an interpretation whose warrant should remain visible in the data. Fluent codes and plausible themes are easy for an LLM to generate; establishing methodological congruence, evidential sufficiency, representativeness, reflexivity, and safety is much harder.

The reviewed literature spans different tasks and epistemologies. Some papers predict a short label for a presegmented quotation; others apply a fixed codebook, induce a hierarchy, create themes, build an interactive workbench, support noncoding narrative synthesis, or evaluate competing code spaces. Treating all of these as one “thematic analysis” benchmark erases the claim being tested. Parfenova et al. evaluate one-code-per-segment generation rather than axial or theme construction ([Parfenova et al., 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.361/)); QuaLLM combines extraction, classification, aggregation, and prevalence estimation ([Rao et al., 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.74/)); NITA treats coding as optional and keeps interpretive synthesis with researchers ([Nguyen, Trung, and Nguyen, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1080/14780887.2026.2638348)). A fixed-label F1 or κ claim can be sound without licensing a claim of equivalent reflexive, grounded-theory, or clinical interpretation.

This review therefore asks both what systems do and what their evidence licenses us to conclude. It covers 181 core computational or empirical reports and 56 adjacent, contextual, and methodological records through 24 August 2026. Preprints, accepted manuscripts, posters, protocols, and position papers are retained where they define the frontier, but are labeled separately from verified peer-reviewed work. Publication families are counted once at the preferred verified version, with event-year, online-first, preprint, and formal-proceedings histories preserved where they differ.

# 2. Review questions

The review addresses eight questions.

1. **RQ1—tasks and methods:** Which parts of familiarization, quotation selection, open or deductive coding, codebook induction/refinement, axial or hierarchical coding, noncoding theme development, subtheme/theme creation, evidence retrieval, memo/schema construction, conceptual modeling, reporting, confidence/abstention, stopping, and quality assurance are automated or assisted?
2. **RQ2—methodological grounding:** Does each study claim and operationalize reflexive TA, codebook TA, coding-reliability TA, grounded theory, qualitative content analysis, Framework analysis, rapid analysis, noncoding qualitative synthesis, generic coding, or topic discovery? Where does the implemented operation narrow or conflict with the label?
3. **RQ3—architecture:** Which model versions, prompt/adaptation strategies, agents or ensembles, retrieval and graph components, chunking/context strategies, stochastic controls, confidence/diversity routes, evidence links, stopping rules, and intervention points form the workflow?
4. **RQ4—human–AI relationship:** Is the human absent after setup, a question/codebook supplier, a reference creator, a post-hoc checker, an iterative collaborator, an adjudicator, a domain or stakeholder validator, or the primary analyst with authority to reject, backtrack, memo, and stop?
5. **RQ5—evaluation:** What construct does each automatic or human operation purport to measure; is it adequately operationalized, independently validated, compatible with plurality, calibrated, source-grounded, and statistically supported; and does workload include verification?
6. **RQ6—validity threats:** Could results arise from participant, source, site, diagnosis, platform, language, time, boilerplate, interviewer speech, length/position, overlap/leakage, contamination, lexical style, same-family judging, anchoring, majority dominance, overconfidence, or omitted negative cases?
7. **RQ7—ethics:** Are consent, ethics review, privacy, deployment, retention/training terms, bias, stakeholder involvement, credential/release security, accountability, and reproducibility restrictions reported?
8. **RQ8—opportunity:** Which gaps remain after version-family deduplication and full-text inspection of the 2025–2026 wave, and which can support a defensible October ARR paper?

# 3. Search and screening methodology

## 3.1 Scope, protocol, and eligibility

Eligible primary work presents, evaluates, or substantively analyzes computational support for qualitative coding or interpretation; proposes evaluation directly applicable to codes or themes; studies human–AI qualitative analysis; or isolates a validity mechanism that changes such evaluation. Methodological sources were retained to assess congruence. Pure topic modeling, summarization, sentiment analysis, or fixed-label prediction without a directly relevant qualitative contribution; studies that merely use manual TA to study another technology; commercial pages; and unverifiable records were excluded from the core. Adjacent records were retained when they supplied directly relevant evidence about constructs, confounding, privacy, or workflow design.

The search began with ten specified seeds and five supplied works and added backward references, focused forward-title/citation queries, same-author and publication-family searches, and searches for systems, datasets, and metrics cited by eligible studies. A preprint and proceedings or journal paper were treated as one family unless their methods materially differed. DeTAILS remains a material exception: the seven-page CUI paper and its expanded preprint have different evaluative content ([official CUI paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/3719160.3735657); [expanded preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17575)). Citation chaining was focused and source coverage differed; no exhaustive citation-saturation claim is made.

# 4. Conceptual foundations of thematic analysis

“Thematic analysis” is a family, not a single algorithm. Coding-reliability TA usually applies a comparatively structured frame and can make agreement and adjudication appropriate. Codebook TA uses a structured coding framework while retaining interpretive judgment. Reflexive TA treats researcher subjectivity and iterative engagement as analytic resources; themes are constructed patterns of shared meaning, not labels waiting to be recovered. Braun and Clarke explicitly warn against one-size-fits-all quality criteria and against importing consensus coding or saturation into reflexive TA as universal tests ([Braun and Clarke, 2021a](https://doi.org/10.1080/14780887.2020.1769238); [Braun and Clarke, 2021b](https://doi.org/10.1002/capr.12360)).

Grounded theory requires more than “open” and “axial” labels: constant comparison, memoing, theoretical sampling, category integration, and theory development are central, with axial coding belonging to some schools rather than all ([Corbin and Strauss, 1990](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00988593)). Qualitative content analysis can be conventional, directed, or summative, which changes whether categories arise from the data, prior theory, or counted content plus contextual interpretation ([Hsieh and Shannon, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1177/1049732305276687)). Framework analysis preserves a case-by-code matrix and is well suited to transparent team comparison ([Gale et al., 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3848812/)). Rapid qualitative analysis uses focused templates and matrices for time-sensitive questions; its quality depends on purpose, expertise, omissions, and verification, not speed alone ([PARRQA, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11468362/)).

These distinctions matter computationally. A model that predicts a short code for each sentence can be evaluated as labeling. A model that constructs latent themes in RTA cannot be validated solely by matching one analyst's wording. A grounded-theory system should expose comparisons, memos, category relations, and sampling decisions rather than merely cluster open codes. A Framework tool should retain case context and matrix provenance. The common pipeline—segment text, generate labels, embed or LLM-merge them, compare the output with a consensus set—is usually most congruent with coding-reliability or codebook TA, even when a paper invokes reflexive language.

This is not a purely terminological objection. McDonald, Schoenebeck, and Forte found substantial method-label ambiguity in 251 CSCW papers: only 20 of 85 papers reporting open coding described grounded theory, and fewer than half of the 161 papers coding to identify themes named an analysis method ([McDonald et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1145/3359174)). **Reviewer interpretation:** computational papers inherit this ambiguity and then add a measurement error: they often translate “similar to a reference codebook” into “valid qualitative interpretation.” The appropriate remedy is an explicit analytic contract stating the research question, unit, tradition, claim level, human role, expected product, and success criteria before running the model.

## 4.1 Claimed versus operationalized methodology in the core corpus

The claimed-versus-operationalized audit was recomputed over all 181 core records in the frozen master. Both fields contain substantive information for 169 records; ten report neither clearly enough to code; `ashwin_2025_serious_bias` contains a claim without an adequately specified operation, and `bai_2026_three_datasets` contains an operational description without a clear tradition claim. These are reporting-coverage counts, not congruence scores. A nonempty “thematic analysis” field does not show that the implementation follows the named tradition.

A strict lower-bound reviewer audit flagged 34 of 181 core reports as containing a material claim–operation mismatch or narrowing. The rule did **not** flag sparse reporting or weak evaluation alone, and it did not flag fixed-label classification when a paper explicitly claimed deductive coding. Each flagged record was assigned one dominant category to avoid double counting. The 34/181 figure is therefore a reproducible reviewer judgment under this rubric, not an estimate of author error or a timeless field prevalence.

The audit also identifies comparatively congruent patterns. Bounded codebook and qualitative-content-analysis studies can appropriately target code assignment, agreement, or downstream estimates if the units and labels are independently justified. NITA is congruent with its noncoding, researcher-primary claim because positionality, counterevidence, theme retention, and the final synthesis remain human responsibilities ([Nguyen, Trung, and Nguyen, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1080/14780887.2026.2638348)). Reflexis operationalizes several reflexive commitments through optional prompts, preserved disagreement, immutable code histories, and analyst control, although its short, synthetic-corpus study does not establish improved interpretation ([Ye et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791275)). Ronaghi et al. likewise match their task-specific claim to a human-final workflow: models organize researcher-authored summaries in one task and retrieve, verify, and sort quote-grounded evidence across 167 interviews in another; humans retain contextual interpretation and intervention decisions ([Ronaghi et al., 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14478)).

**RQ2 answer.** The corpus includes reflexive, codebook, and coding-reliability TA; grounded-theory claims; qualitative content, Framework, and rapid-analysis variants; generic open or deductive coding; topic/concept discovery; and deliberate noncoding synthesis. Their success criteria are not interchangeable. Fixed-label F1 or κ supports a bounded assignment claim; cluster coherence supports organization; reference similarity supports proximity to one reading; none alone establishes reflexive interpretation, grounded-theory development, or clinically warranted themes. The complete 181-row extraction preserves each author's label beside the reviewer's operational description so readers can audit this distinction paper by paper.

# 5. Taxonomy of computational approaches

## 5.1 Historical trajectory

Pre-LLM systems used co-occurrence, latent semantic analysis, topic models, embeddings, clustering, and supervised classifiers to organize text. The conceptual break introduced by instruction-tuned LLMs is not that clustering disappeared; rather, natural-language generation could draft codes, definitions, evidence rationales, and hierarchical relations while accepting research questions and codebooks in ordinary language.

The resulting literature is better represented by ten nonexclusive operational families. A single system can occupy several families; the list is a task map, not ten mutually exclusive paper bins.

1. **Fixed-label or codebook assignment.** Dai et al. co-construct and then apply a codebook; Muse, QuaLLM, Turner et al., Than et al., and many applied studies assign defined codes to segments ([Dai et al., 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.669/); [Matveyenko et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-industry.131/); [Rao et al., 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.74/); [Turner et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s43058-026-00953-8); [Than et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/00491241251339188)). This is the regime in which F1, accuracy, or κ has the clearest target, provided prevalence, units, and independent labels are reported.
2. **Open-code suggestion and collaborative code development.** Qualitative Code Suggestion, CollabCoder, Dai, DeTAILS, LLMCode, MindCoder, and ROSAnnotator expose candidate codes for human acceptance, editing, rejection, or comparison ([Spinoso et al., 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.993/); [Gao et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642002); [Sharma and Wallace, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3719160.3735657); [Oksanen et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16671); [Gao et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00775); [Zhang et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/HRI61500.2025.10974254)). Suggestion uptake is not independent validity because seeing a candidate can anchor the analyst.
3. **Hierarchy, schema, axial, and theory-like refinement.** HICode, LOGOS, Neo-Grounded Theory, and newer open–axial–selective or taxonomy systems merge codes, relate categories, or optimize schemas ([Zhong et al., 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1580/); [Pi et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24294); [Wen et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25244); [Liu et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28889)). These operations implement structure; without theoretical sampling, constant comparison, memo integration, and evaluated theory development, they do not establish grounded-theory automation.
4. **Code-first end-to-end theme pipelines.** LLM-TA, Auto-TA, SFT-TA, TAMA, and clinical refinement systems sequence quotation/code generation, consolidation, themes, evaluation, and report-like outputs ([Raza et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01620); [Yi et al., 2025, Auto-TA preprint/poster](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23998); [Yi et al., 2025, SFT-TA preprint/non-archival poster](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17167); [Xu et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3828752); [Yi et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08989)). An end-to-end artifact chain is not evidence of end-to-end interpretive validity.
5. **Noncoding narrative and theme development.** NITA explicitly makes coding optional and keeps positionality, counterevidence, theme retention, and the meta-narrative with researchers ([Nguyen, Trung, and Nguyen, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1080/14780887.2026.2638348)). This family matters conceptually because “theme generation” need not be implemented as clustering increasingly abstract labels.
6. **Interactive workbenches and reflexivity support.** [LLooM](https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642830), [DeTAILS](https://doi.org/10.1145/3719160.3735657), [Muse](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-industry.131/), [CentaurTA](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.778/), [Co-Refine](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19309), [Reflexis](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791275), and [Teleoscope](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791310) provide editable concepts, persistent feedback, drift/disagreement prompts, or branchable analytic graphs. Reflexis shows that deliberation and positionality interfaces exist; the open question is whether they improve independently judged interpretation over time.
7. **Autonomous, role-prompted, and deliberative pipelines.** [Thematic-LM](https://doi.org/10.1145/3696410.3714595), [TA-MAS](https://doi.org/10.35542/osf.io/kq8zh_v1), [Auto-TA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23998), [TAMA](https://doi.org/10.1145/3828752), and related systems assign coder, aggregator, reviewer, or critic roles. “Multi-agent” can mean one model repeated under role prompts, same-model deliberation, or genuinely different model families. Borchers et al. found only 5 of 432 reported contrasts favoring multi-agent consensus and 65 favoring single agents, so consensus cannot be assumed to provide independent triangulation ([Borchers et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21039879)).
8. **Adaptation, fine-tuning, and steering.** Parfenova et al. use LoRA for short-code generation; Ganesh et al. show task-specific fine-tuning can outperform in-context learning; SFT-TA trains on paraphrases derived from ten of twelve reference themes; and the AIED confidence study tests activation steering ([Parfenova et al., 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.361/); [Ganesh et al., 2024](https://www.educationaldatamining.org/edm2024/proceedings/2024.EDM-posters.95/); [Yi et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17167); [Li et al., AIED 2026 manuscript/formal 2027 proceedings](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-29755-6_18)). Leakage-safe held-out concepts, domains, model families, and dates are necessary before calling adaptation general.
9. **Retrieval, evidence, provenance, and version control.** [Clinical pipelines](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08989), [Nyaaba](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11850), [MindCoder](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00775), [ROSAnnotator](https://doi.org/10.1109/HRI61500.2025.10974254), [Reflexis](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791275), [Teleoscope](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791310), and [ROCK](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/4q8bs_v2) connect claims to quotations or source spans and increasingly retain revisions and branches. Traceability is an enabling condition; exactness, sufficiency, participant spread, counterevidence, and independent-auditor usability remain separate empirical questions.
10. **Evaluation, confidence, disagreement, and stopping.** Aggregate Code Space, source-symmetric verification, IDEAlign, ICR, LLM-judge calibration, rationale-based triage, repeated-run comparisons, saturation ratios, and refinement trajectories make assurance a first-class computational task ([Chen et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.2073/); [Liu et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28890); [Nam et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02855); [Pérez et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04413); [Han et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00008); [Tajik et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3785022.3785101); [De Paoli and Mathis, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04859)). Severe confidence miscalibration and anchoring show that a numeric or verbal confidence score is not itself a trustworthy routing signal.

**RQ1 answer.** Implemented support now covers familiarization proxies; quotation selection; open and deductive coding; codebook induction, reduction, and refinement; axial/hierarchical grouping; subtheme and theme generation, naming, definition, and refinement; noncoding pattern and narrative development; evidence retrieval and quotation verification; memo/schema construction; report generation; provenance/version control; disagreement and confidence triage; repeated-run comparison; stopping signals; and automatic or human quality assurance. Familiarization is usually summarized or retrieved rather than evaluated as sustained engagement. Conceptual models and theory-like schemas are generated, but inspected evidence does not yet establish full grounded-theory development. Simple no-new-theme, saturation-ratio, fixed-round, iterative-versus-batch, and analyst-confidence stopping signals exist; their held-out relationship to supported minority/negative-case recall and verification burden remains unvalidated.

**RQ3 answer.** Architectures span proprietary and open-weight models, zero/few-shot prompting, LoRA or supervised fine-tuning, retrieval, clustering and graphs, same-model role agents, cross-model ensembles, repeated runs, self/cross critique, confidence routing, and human-editable provenance. Exact snapshots, chunk order, context strategy, seeds, retries, and human edits remain inconsistently reported. A role label does not confer independent expertise, a temperature of zero does not guarantee determinism, and a source link does not establish a warranted or representative interpretation.

# 6. Human–AI collaboration models

“Human in the loop,” “collaboration,” and “automation” describe very different interventions. This review classifies the implemented decision point and authority, not the paper's preferred label.

| Implemented human relationship | Representative systems | What the person actually does | Reviewer assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| No intervention after setup | HICode, Thematic-LM, Auto-TA, automated provenance/refinement | Supplies a goal, background, or starting corpus; an LLM “reviewer” may revise another LLM | Automated for the tested run, not collaborative interpretation ([HICode](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1580/); [Thematic-LM](https://doi.org/10.1145/3696410.3714595); [Auto-TA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23998); [provenance/refinement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08989)). |
| Question, examples, or codebook supplied | QuaLLM, Turner, Muse apply-code, fixed-label studies | Defines the question, categories, decision rules, and sometimes examples | Appropriate bounded assistance if the codebook and evaluation labels are independent; not evidence of inductive theme discovery ([QuaLLM](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.74/); [Turner](https://doi.org/10.1186/s43058-026-00953-8); [Muse](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-industry.131/)). |
| Reference creator or post-hoc checker | Mackenzie, HICode subset, TAMA, Misra | Creates the human reference, judges outputs after generation, or stops refinement | Valuable expertise, but “in the loop” can overstate intervention and reuse of one's own themes reduces independence ([Mackenzie](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19775); [HICode](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1580/); [TAMA](https://doi.org/10.1145/3828752); [Misra et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1177/16094069261426100)). |
| Reviews or edits proposals | Dai, LLooM, DeTAILS, HHitL-TA, LLMCode, MindCoder, ROSAnnotator, CentaurTA | Accepts, rejects, merges, revises, explains, backtracks, or stops | Substantive control is stronger than post-hoc checking, but exposure to machine suggestions can anchor the human; pre/post artifacts and rejected alternatives should be logged ([Dai](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.669/); [LLooM](https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642830); [DeTAILS](https://doi.org/10.1145/3719160.3735657); [HHitL-TA](https://repository.isls.org/handle/1/11227); [LLMCode](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16671); [MindCoder](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00775); [ROSAnnotator](https://doi.org/10.1109/HRI61500.2025.10974254); [CentaurTA](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.778/)). |
| Pair or team deliberation | Reflexis, Nyaaba | Compares disagreements, receives optional reflexivity prompts, writes memos, revises interpretations, and can ignore AI advice | Reflexis preserves disagreement and control; Nyaaba documents staged Ghanaian researcher chats, revisions, and memos. Neither by itself proves improved downstream validity ([Reflexis](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791275); [Nyaaba](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11850)). |
| Independent adjudicator or domain expert | Hill; blind source-symmetric K–12 comparison; specialist clinical comparisons | Judges evidence or competing outputs while blinded where possible | Stronger when reference creation is separated, expertise and sampling are explicit, and disagreement/uncertainty are retained ([Hill](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pdig.0001189); [Agreement Is Not Quality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28890); [specialist/lay clinical comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19008)). |
| Participant or stakeholder validator | Rare; some source analyses were previously member-checked | Assesses whether an AI-assisted interpretation fits lived experience or consequences | An inherited member check validates the source analysis, not a later model output ([Zhu et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.591/)). AI-specific participant/stakeholder validation remains uncommon among inspected full texts. |
| Human remains primary analyst | NITA, Vikan, Ronaghi | Owns positionality, counterevidence, interpretive synthesis, and final decisions; the model organizes, retrieves, suggests, or verifies bounded artifacts | Most congruent with reflexive or high-stakes interpretation, but may save no time and still requires audits for anchoring and omission ([NITA](https://doi.org/10.1080/14780887.2026.2638348); [Vikan](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12949038/); [Ronaghi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14478)). |

The expanded DeTAILS preprint reports high suggestion-to-revision F1. That can indicate useful alignment, but without a no-suggestion arm it cannot distinguish assistance from researchers retaining the machine's framing ([Sharma et al., 2025 expanded preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17575)). CentaurTA makes feedback persistent, yet scores can decline after repeated updates, so “self-improving” is a mechanism with local evidence, not monotonic general learning ([Wang et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.778/)). Reflexis reports that 11/12 researchers valued its disagreement focus and 12/12 felt they retained control, while its two author-raters judged all seven triggered drift alerts and 18 prompts relevant; the lack of a baseline, synthetic corpus, and nonindependent ratings limits outcome claims ([Ye et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791275)).

The K–12 instrument pipeline provides a procedural counterexample to token oversight. Researchers read raw data beside model output, memoed misleading or generic proposals, retained every merge/category decision, and then three trained coders applied the resulting 67-item hierarchy to a message- and conversation-disjoint 2,560-message sample and added five missed codes ([Liu et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28889)). Its broader corpus contains 45,000 messages; the inductive phases used 21,147 overlapping three-message windows. These denominators describe different analytic stages and must not be interchanged.

**RQ4 answer.** Human roles range from absent after setup, through question/codebook supply and post-hoc reference checking, to iterative editing, adjudication, pair deliberation, domain validation, and researcher-primary interpretation. Claims of collaboration are accurate only when a person can intervene in the live analysis and retains consequential authority. The most defensible workflows expose machine suggestions and evidence, log edits/rejections/rationales, preserve disagreement, permit abstention and backtracking, and separate reference creators from evaluators where feasible.

# 7. Datasets and application domains

The empirical base is broader by document count than by qualitative setting. Large corpora include Reddit, forum posts, news, litigation/OCR segments, academic abstracts, and survey responses; health work adds interviews and focus groups that are smaller but methodologically consequential. HICode spans four corpora from 369 documents to 163,173 OCR segments ([Zhong et al., 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1580/)). QuaLLM processes 65,377 Reddit submissions and 1,392,776 comments, retaining 58,728 concerns after an 11% API-failure rate ([Rao et al., 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.74/)). Muse releases an eleven-dataset evaluation collection spanning interviews, news, academic/medical abstracts, social media, legal transcripts, and discussion data ([Matveyenko et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-industry.131/)). These studies establish computational scale, but arXiv category labels or Reddit topics are not a proxy for latent interpretation of a sensitive interview.

The AAOCA family is the most important dependency correction in the evidence base. LLM-TA, Auto-TA, SFT-TA, TAMA, and the 2026 refinement/provenance paper share the same nine focus groups and twelve published themes. Their pipeline comparisons are useful, but improvements cannot be counted as independent external validation. SFT-TA further converts ten of those themes into 300 paraphrased training examples before evaluating against the twelve-theme universe ([Yi et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17167)). The later provenance paper expands to five corpora, but its clinical human-theme comparisons still inherit AAOCA/SVCHD references and its best iteration is chosen using a composite on held-out test material ([Yi et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08989)).

Other dependence families also constrain apparent replication breadth. [NITA](https://doi.org/10.1080/14780887.2026.2638348) extends GAITA on the same Down East corpus and question; [TA-MAS](https://doi.org/10.35542/osf.io/kq8zh_v1) and [Simon's human-versus-MAS comparison](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-98420-4_5) share the Simon/CSCL survey and human analysis; the [QualiGPT preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14925) and [later reliability study](https://doi.org/10.54941/ahfe1006232) form a dependent system family; and De Paoli's [Italian](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08488) and [saturation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04859) studies extend a staged workflow. Several applied papers also reanalyze earlier human-coded corpora or inherit published themes. These records remain distinct when they contribute a new evaluation or workflow question, but they are not counted as independent dataset replications. The publication-family ledger in Appendix 19.2 preserves the preferred version and dependencies.

Language and demographic coverage remain uneven, not absent. The inspected corpus includes Finnish political text, Italian and German interviews, Dutch news, Japanese clinical interviews, translated Kenyan focus groups and Rohingya/host interviews, Ghanaian researcher deliberation, Korean/Chinese/Japanese social-media data, and English varieties including African American English. Kempny et al. nevertheless found that 93% of the 75 studies in their pre-May-2025 health-methods review used GPT-family models and identified limited non-English evidence ([Kempny et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12874-026-02913-1)). The most diagnostic later studies suggest that ordinary English manifest content is the easier regime. Japanese culturally specific categories, translated refugee interviews, AAE, trauma narratives, facilitator speech, and rare clinical experiences produce qualitatively different errors ([Sakaguchi et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.2196/71521); [Ashwin et al., 2025/2026](https://doi.org/10.1177/00491241251338246); [Zhu et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.591/); [Hill et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pdig.0001189)).

Specialized deductive tasks also resist transfer from general demonstrations. In a peer-reviewed ICPC 2026 study, four frontier/open models apply nine security-specific codes to 263 human experiment comments; even a refined prompt yields average chance-corrected performance in the limited range (GPT-5 κ/adjusted accuracy .26 and Claude-4 .30), and added prompt detail does not produce a Bonferroni-significant improvement ([Camporese et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3794763.3798172)). This is a coding task rather than theme induction, but it directly warns against claims that a generally strong model transfers to technical qualitative codes without domain validation.

# 8. Evaluation practices

## 8.1 Automatic metrics and their constructs

The central evaluation rule is to separate the construct, its operationalization, and evidence that the operation is valid. This follows the measurement framework of Jacobs and Wallach, which treats construct definition and measurement-model validity as prior to numerical comparison ([Jacobs and Wallach, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1145/3442188.3445901)).

| Operation | Plausible construct | What it cannot establish alone | Evidence needed before strong use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exact/fuzzy match; ROUGE, BLEU, METEOR | Surface similarity to a selected reference | Interpretive equivalence, evidence adequacy, reflexivity, uniqueness | Human code/theme judgments, unmatched-output accounting, configuration sensitivity ([ROUGE](https://aclanthology.org/W04-1013/)) |
| BERTScore/cosine/learned semantic metric | Proximity in a model-specific representation | Same analytic claim, warrant, polarity, participant base, or theory | Expert validation on code/theme pairs and split/merge sets; threshold transfer ([BERTScore](https://openreview.net/forum?id=SkeHuCVFDr); [MoverScore](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1053/)) |
| One-to-one bipartite/Hungarian matching | Parsimonious alignment at similar granularity | Legitimate many-to-one or one-to-many relations | Granularity-stratified analysis and unmatched false-positive penalties |
| Many-to-many threshold matching | Flexible recovery under splits/merges | Valid embedding space or threshold | Threshold sensitivity, expert mapping, contradiction probes |
| Reference coverage/recall | Recovery of content in one reference | Content missed by all references; participant spread; interpretation quality | Multiple independent readings plus participant/evidence audit |
| Novelty/divergence | Difference from a pooled/reference code space | Whether difference is insight or unsupported invention | Source fidelity and qualified human adjudication |
| Coherence/distinctiveness | Within-theme homogeneity/between-theme separation | Analytic depth; a tension-bearing theme may be heterogeneous | Expert ratings of central concept, boundaries, and counterevidence |
| Repeated-run similarity | Procedural stability under tested variation | Validity; output can be stably shallow or biased | Crossed perturbations and substantive adjudication |
| Provenance/quote links | Traceability and auditability | Correct, representative, sufficient interpretation | Exactness, entailment, omission, participant spread, and audit-usability tests |
| Cohen/Fleiss κ, alpha, Gwet AC1 | Reliability of defined judgments under prevalence/design assumptions | Truth or quality across all traditions | Units, prevalence, marginals, disagreements, expertise, intervals |
| LLM-as-judge | Agreement with a judge's rubric/application | Independent human validity | Cross-family judges, random order, repetition, expert calibration, error analysis |

Parfenova et al.'s human study gives a compact example of metric–construct divergence. BERTScore favors closeness to the reference, yet three researchers evaluating fifteen items sometimes preferred human alternatives with greater analytic depth; Krippendorff's alpha was only .20 ([Parfenova et al., 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.361/)). HICode uses many-to-many matching at several cosine thresholds, a better topology for legitimate splits and merges than forced one-to-one matching, but two original annotators produced human precision/recall different from cosine results and agreed with alpha .31 ([Zhong et al., 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1580/)). Dörfel and Ammoneit's full-text comparison makes the divergence especially concrete: 13 of 14 LLM-derived codes cleared a .60 semantic-similarity threshold against human codes and repeated applications reached mean Jaccard .83, yet global coding-unit overlap with the human analysis was only .39 and the LLM code system lost domain-specific distinctions ([Dörfel and Ammoneit, 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci16081314)). These are not reasons to abandon semantic matching; they are reasons to validate it on the intended interpretive task and at the unit where the analytic claim is made.

**Reviewer interpretation among the inspected full texts:** Chen et al. provide the most developed no-single-gold diagnostic in this evidence map. Four human and four differently prompted Gemma-3-27B coders generate codebooks for 127 community messages; an aggregate code space supports Coverage, Overlap, Novelty, and Jensen–Shannon Divergence, with ten runs used for stability. The authors explicitly frame the measures as a profile rather than a universal score. Their simulations also reveal failure: code flooding can improve Coverage, Overlap, and Novelty, while concepts omitted by every coder remain outside the aggregate space ([Chen et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.2073/)). The method replaces one gold codebook with a team-relative universe, an important but incomplete step toward plurality.

Among the inspected full texts, the July 2026 *Agreement Is Not Quality* preprint supplies a substantial source-symmetric verification. Five LLMs and three trained humans independently apply a 72-item, multi-label K–12 codebook to 2,560 messages. An independent domain expert, blind to source, judges 855 paired code sets. Mean human–LLM Jaccard is .30 versus approximately .52 human–human, yet among decisive human–LLM comparisons the expert prefers humans 51.5% and LLMs 48.5% (p=.537); a Bradley–Terry model interleaves humans and models. This directly demonstrates that agreement and blind expert preference can diverge in both directions ([Liu et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28890)). Its boundary is equally important: one verifier, one codebook, one platform, a selected model set, and code assignment rather than inductive theme interpretation. **Reviewer interpretation:** a generic “agreement is not quality” contribution is already occupied within this evidence map; the open advance is multi-expert, multi-domain, provenance-aware validation at code and theme levels.

## 8.2 Human evaluation

Human protocols vary from one expert inspecting one transcript to blinded multi-analyst comparisons. TAMA's expert is clinically qualified but helped create the original reference themes; five lay reviewers are mentioned without enough procedure to support an independent qualitative-validity claim ([Xu et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3828752)). SFT-TA uses four blinded raters—one surgeon, one qualitative analyst, and two laypeople—but does not report inter-rater reliability ([Yi et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17167)). Parfenova and Pfeffer correlate a composite metric with human scores on only ten items and do not report the number of experts ([Parfenova and Pfeffer, 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.563/)).

Later designs fill only parts of the gap. Reflexis observes twelve experienced researchers in six pairs, but two authors rate prompt relevance and there is no baseline ([Ye et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791275)). Han et al. calibrate an LLM judge on only 60 of 3,560 outputs; the five-model aggregate makes rank correlations unstable ([Han et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00008)). ICR uses trained human ratings to expose meaning-level disagreements hidden by cosine/F1, but a position paper and preprint family should not be treated as a settled universal metric ([Pérez et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.gem-main.81/); [ICR preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04413)). Ronaghi et al. compare manual and LLM-assisted work before scale-up and retain contextual interpretation with researchers; its practitioner-usefulness evidence is stronger than pure similarity, but the evaluators are the workflow's developers and the report remains a preprint ([Ronaghi et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14478)).

Hill et al. offer a stronger design: human analysts and ChatGPT-5, Claude 4 Sonnet, and QualiGPT were mutually blinded, outputs required quotations and segment identifiers, facilitator segments were excluded, and error was assessed under strict and broader definitions. Yet the study contains one two-hour focus group with seven adults, so the design is more persuasive than the generality ([Hill et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pdig.0001189)). Zhu et al. partner with firearm-violence researchers, build the human codes through grounded-theory/inductive procedures, conduct member checking for the source analysis, and maintain local processing; however, the scale of machine outputs makes full human validation infeasible, motivating embedding thresholds and sampled audits ([Zhu et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.591/)).

A minimally adequate human evaluation should report evaluator count and expertise, independence from reference creation, blinding, randomized order, sampled units and rationale, rubric wording, independent versus consensus ratings, disagreement/reliability with uncertainty, statistical unit, multiplicity, and time. The field's reporting problem is not unique: NLG reviews document inconsistent quality terms and frequent omissions of evaluators, sampling, rating design, and statistics ([van der Lee et al., 2019](https://aclanthology.org/W19-8643/); [Howcroft et al., 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.inlg-1.23/)).

## 8.3 Cost and workload

Runtime is routinely compared with human coding hours, but verification is often excluded. QuaLLM reports 145.49 million tokens and a cost of $1,662.30 ([Rao et al., 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.74/)). TAMA reports machine processing in minutes versus roughly 30 hours per human coder on AAOCA ([Xu et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3828752)). Turner et al. estimate 97% time and 99% cost reductions for deductive dementia-trial coding, but 42.6% of 929 extracted excerpts were incorrect, making uncounted adjudication material ([Turner et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s43058-026-00953-8)). Ronaghi et al. report 30% and 55% time reductions for two researchers on the site-summary task and scale quote-grounded analysis to 167 interviews, while also documenting retrieval fragmentation, contextual correction, and continuing human synthesis ([Ronaghi et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14478)). Vikan et al.'s rigorous offline reflexive use found no time saving and documented fabricated quotations and changed meanings ([Vikan et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12949038/)). Efficiency claims should include cleaning, segmentation, prompt/codebook development, retries, source verification, false-positive review, privacy review, documentation, and reruns after model drift.

**RQ5 answer.** Evaluation ranges from exact/fuzzy matching, F1/accuracy, κ/alpha, lexical and embedding similarity, one-to-one or many-to-many matching, coverage/novelty/coherence, repeated-run stability, and LLM judging to blinded expert comparison, quotation audits, participant/source coverage, downstream estimates, workload, and cost. No single operation validates all qualitative claims. Adequacy depends on the declared construct, independent evaluators, plurality assumptions, source-level error categories, representative sampling, uncertainty and statistical units, calibration, and the verification work required to make outputs safe.

# 9. Validity, reliability, and interpretability

## 9.1 A reference interpretation is not objective ground truth

Human themes are evidence, but their epistemic role depends on the method. In coding-reliability designs, agreement with a carefully developed codebook can be the target. In reflexive TA, a reference is one situated reading. Several studies acknowledge this yet still organize quantitative evaluation around a consensus set. TAMA calls its twelve themes reliable ground truth while later noting that thematic interpretations are not unique ([Xu et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3828752)). Dai's LLM participates in constructing the final codebook, so subsequent agreement is partly a compatibility test rather than independent discovery ([Dai et al., 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.669/)). A valid plural design should preserve alternative codes, rationales, and disagreements rather than collapse them before evaluation.

## 9.2 Reliability is conditional, not validity

Repeated-run similarity, inter-rater agreement, and consistent code assignment are valuable. They answer whether a procedure behaves similarly under specified conditions. They do not show that the stable output is warranted. Auto-TA's bidirectional ROUGE across ten runs can identify lexical instability, but cannot establish qualitative dependability ([Yi et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23998)). Bijker et al. obtain substantial agreement across multiple ChatGPT conversations in content analysis, yet that alone cannot reveal shared bias or systematic misreading ([Bijker et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.2196/59050)). Hill et al.'s 93% agreement paired with κ .34 illustrates prevalence sensitivity, while Gwet AC1 remained about .92–.93 ([Hill et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pdig.0001189)). Reporting only the most favorable reliability coefficient invites misinterpretation.

Confidence is a separate construct and cannot repair this problem by assertion. In the AIED coding study, mean verbal confidence near .9 accompanies accuracy of .338–.520 and ECE of .403–.553; a .95 anchor is copied in 96.5% of outputs and leaves discrimination near chance (AUC .518) ([Li et al., 2026/2027](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-29755-6_18)). Rationale similarity can prioritize disagreements for review, but Tajik et al.'s large rationale-similarity difference (`d=1.16`) is a triage signal, not evidence that either agent is correct ([Tajik et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3785022.3785101)).

## 9.3 Abstraction is not automatically quality

Systems often reward concise, nonredundant, semantically coherent themes. But high abstraction can erase cases, and embedding homogeneity can favor broad topics over interpretive themes that connect tension. In Zhu et al., BERTopic reduced thousands of labels to fewer than sixty, increasing apparent relevance but reducing formal-human-code capture and producing some tenuous, stereotypical, or unsupported labels ([Zhu et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.591/)). In LLM-TA, a rare cardiac arrest can become disproportionately salient ([Raza et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01620)). Conversely, topic models of autistic/ADHD youth interviews underrepresented or missed masking, self-doubt, and strengths, and higher-Jaccard iterative analysis still missed low-frequency concerns ([Lukito et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-34570-7); [Logan et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772363.3798789)). Theme count, hierarchical depth, parsimony, and coherence should therefore be treated as design properties, not quality outcomes.

## 9.4 Interpretability requires warrants

A theme name and several relevant quotations are inspectable, but an interpretive warrant must explain why those passages support that theme, how contrary passages were handled, and whose accounts are represented. A same-family judge can check surface consistency without revealing shared assumptions. TAMA's one-transcript GPT-4o-mini audit found no hallucinated themes and high grounding for GPT-4o output, but the authors acknowledge circularity and the limited audit ([Xu et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3828752)). The 2026 trauma study is more revealing because source-linked examination differentiates likely valid novel codes from tenuous “reading between the lines” and unsupported stereotypes ([Zhu et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.591/)).

# 10. Provenance, auditability, and reproducibility

Provenance has evolved from optional quotations to structured lineage. Official paper records and their linked artifacts document resources for Dai ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.669/); [code](https://github.com/sjdai/LLM-thematic-analysis)), LLooM ([paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642830); [code](https://github.com/michelle123lam/lloom)), HICode ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1580/); [code](https://github.com/mianzg/HICode)), DeTAILS ([paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/3719160.3735657); [code](https://github.com/DaemonOnCode/DeTAILS)), CentaurTA ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.778/); [code](https://github.com/Tom-Owl/CentaurTA)), TAMA ([paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/3828752); [code](https://github.com/Charlie-Yi-SJ/TAMA)), LLM-TA ([preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01620); [code](https://github.com/jiaweixu98/LLM-TA)), Muse ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-industry.131/); [evaluation data](https://github.com/jdmatv/muse-eval-dataset)), and the trauma pipeline ([paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.591/); [code](https://github.com/jhzsquared/AIvsHumanCoding)). **Reviewer judgment among the inspected full texts:** no single implementation dominates every provenance layer. The 2026 clinical refinement preprint carries stable turn IDs through chunks, quotations, normalized codes, typed relations, subthemes, themes, and LLM-reviewer revisions ([Yi et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08989)); Reflexis retains immutable code histories and disagreement/positionality events ([Ye et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791275)); Teleoscope provides branchable graph histories and analyst comparison ([Lee et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791310)); and ROCK specifies interoperable source spans, codebooks, relations, and versions ([Peters et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/4q8bs_v2)).

Logging is not validation. The provenance paper uses GPT-4o-mini in generation and evaluation roles and reports no independent human validation of the ledger's interpretive warrants; public code, complete prompts, and generated ledgers were not located in the inspected version ([Yi et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08989)). Auto-TA has quote IDs but maps the fraction of used quotes to “credibility/confirmability,” which rewards reuse without checking representativeness ([Yi et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23998)). [Reflexis](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791275) validates prompt relevance and perceived control, not whether its histories improve evidential sufficiency; [Teleoscope](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791310) reports analyst confidence in saturation, not held-out coverage; [ROCK](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/4q8bs_v2) is a specification rather than an adoption/outcome study. A theme can be perfectly traceable to one participant and still misrepresent the population.

A provenance-aware evaluation should test five layers separately: (1) **exactness**—does the cited string occur and retain speaker/context? (2) **entailment/warrant**—does it support the stated code/theme? (3) **coverage**—which relevant passages and participants are omitted? (4) **distribution**—is evidence concentrated in a few speakers/sites? and (5) **audit usability**—can an independent analyst reproduce the path, inspect versions, and register disagreement? Stratified human audits should sample high/low-frequency themes, negative cases, early/middle/late context positions, and every participant stratum.

Computational reproducibility remains limited by proprietary drift. Exact dated model identifiers, temperature, top-p, prompt text, context/chunking, retry policy, seeds or run IDs, tool versions, human edits, and stopping rules are often incomplete. Kempny et al. found that 45% of 75 studies did not specify deployment configuration, 75% reported no parameter settings, 61% supplied at least partial prompts, and 13% supplied no prompting details ([Kempny et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12874-026-02913-1)). Restricted qualitative data can be ethically necessary, but papers can still release synthetic fixtures, schemas, prompt/configuration files, hashes, transformation logs, and a runnable audit harness.

# 11. Confounding, identity leakage, and shortcut learning

The expanded corpus contains direct confound and positionality evidence, so a claim that such tests are absent is no longer defensible. Lukito et al. find that apparent diagnosis clustering in autistic/ADHD interview topics is confounded by interviewer identity and is not robust after adjustment ([Lukito et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-34570-7)). Subbiah et al. cross race/gender conditions, three open models, and five seeds on 154 life narratives and observe demographic-conditioned semantic, affective, and thematic shifts, stereotyping, and consequential omissions ([Subbiah et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20131)). Ashwin et al. regress coding errors on respondent attributes: GPT-3.5 errors are associated at the 5% level with characteristics for 10 of 19 codes, versus 1 of 19 for a bespoke classifier, and downstream regression estimates can be distorted ([Ashwin et al., 2025/2026](https://doi.org/10.1177/00491241251338246)). Yu, Liu, and He find much higher within-company than cross-company similarity in earnings-call representations and strong alignment with a ticker-level historical baseline, showing how “text signal” can be repeated-source identity ([Yu et al., 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.946/)). These results demonstrate specific failure mechanisms; they do not prove shortcut use in every theme generator.

Several pathways are visible. Mathis et al. merge interviewer and interviewee speech, making facilitator language inseparable from respondent content ([Mathis et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmpb.2024.108356)). Hill et al. explicitly exclude facilitator segments yet report that facilitator material drives much of the comprehensive error when systems mishandle source boundaries ([Hill et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pdig.0001189)). Disease-background prompts in clinical studies can turn diagnosis language into a shortcut. Random chunk splits can leak the same participant or interviewer across train and test. Elangovan, He, and Verspoor show the general effect of input overlap: in SST-2, accuracy rises from 85.0 in the least-overlapping stratum to 96.8 in the most-overlapping stratum ([Elangovan et al., 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.eacl-main.113/)).

Adjacent task studies sharpen the mechanism. Rizvi et al. show that keyword and lexical cues are insufficient for nuanced ableism classification; Sakib et al. directly probe spurious correlations in social-determinants extraction; and Wei et al.'s China/Japan/Korea platform comparison entangles platform, language, geography, time, and preprocessing and may remove small minority clusters ([Rizvi et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20500); [Sakib et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00134); [Wei et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1609/icwsm.v20i1.42761)). These are not interchangeable with inductive TA, but they justify subgroup, platform, source, and lexical-shortcut checks in a qualitative pipeline.

Long-context and order effects create a second confound. Evidence near the middle of long contexts can be used less reliably than evidence near the beginning or end ([Liu et al., 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9/)). Few-shot exemplar order can change performance from near state of the art to near random, with good orders failing to transfer ([Lu et al., 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-long.556/)). A fixed temperature of zero does not control model drift, hidden serving nondeterminism, chunk order, exemplar order, or context position.

Contamination is an unresolved ambiguity when public corpora and published human themes are used with closed models. Deng et al.'s TS-Guessing method found that ChatGPT and GPT-4 exactly recovered a missing MMLU option in 52% and 57% of cases, demonstrating that contamination can be probed rather than merely asserted ([Deng et al., 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.482/)). The AAOCA reference themes, source papers, and disease description are public. **Reviewer interpretation:** this creates a memorization risk, not evidence that the systems memorized them. A qualitative benchmark should document availability dates, use fresh/unpublished references where possible, mask disease/site/paper names, test paraphrased labels, insert secret placebos/canaries, and attempt missing-theme guessing before exposing the corpus.

The appropriate confound suite combines participant/interviewer/site/domain-disjoint folds; duplicate and nearest-neighbor analysis; probes for participant/source/site identity in embeddings; ablations of facilitator turns, boilerplate, demographics, disease names, and metadata; counterfactual identity swaps; chunk and codebook-order permutations; evidence-position randomization; and rare/contradictory-case insertion. Report theme quality alongside source-probe accuracy and participant-level residual error. A model that remains accurate only within source should be described as source-conditioned, not generally interpretive.

**RQ6 answer.** Demonstrated or credible threats include participant/interviewer/source identity, demographic conditioning, platform/language/time entanglement, overlapping participants or chunks, codebook/reference leakage, development-set reuse, prevalence and class imbalance, lexical style sensitivity, public-corpus contamination, long-context position and order effects, same-family judging, human anchoring, stable shared bias, majority dominance, and omission or sensational amplification of rare cases. Causal shortcut claims require controlled ablation or counterfactual evidence; otherwise the report labels them risks rather than facts.

# 12. Ethics and high-stakes clinical use

Ethical reporting is uneven even when papers discuss ethics in principle. Kempny et al. found that 95% of their broad sample mentioned ethical considerations and 97% incorporated some human verification, but these aggregate categories do not show whether consent covered vendor processing, whether retention was disabled, or who is accountable for an unsupported clinical theme ([Kempny et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12874-026-02913-1)).

Local inference offers a concrete privacy path. Mathis et al. run a quantized LLaMA-2-70B model on local dual RTX 3090 hardware for protected mental-health interviews ([Mathis et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmpb.2024.108356)). Vikan et al. use offline Mistral-7B, report ethics references and consent that explicitly covers LLM use, and retain human authorship/accountability ([Vikan et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12949038/)). Grover et al. use local Hermes-3-Llama-3.1-70B on five Canadian focus groups and keep data access under research-ethics control ([Grover et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pdig.0001263)). Zhu et al. run local open models, withhold survivor transcripts, and manually check that released codes are not identifying ([Zhu et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.591/)).

De-identification is not authorization and does not eliminate risk. Baroud et al. annotate 6,199 indirect identifiers across 100 MIMIC-III notes, showing how contextual combinations remain sensitive ([Baroud et al., 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.privatenlp-main.7/)). Morris et al. reconstruct 92% of 32-token inputs exactly from embeddings and recover 89% of full names from MIMIC-note embeddings, so hosted embedding APIs and vector stores require raw-text-level governance ([Morris et al., 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.765/)). A defensible paper should state the ethics determination, consent and secondary-use scope, whether LLM processing was disclosed, explicit and indirect de-identification, deployment and processor/subprocessor, region, encryption, retention, training use, deletion verification, access control, and incident response.

The expanded evidence also shows why provider assurances and reproducibility artifacts require inspection. Mehta et al. send translated, sensitive focus-group material through an API/Colab workflow; the paper's assurance about provider access is broader than what it documents ([Mehta et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-18969-w)). Frontier privacy policies are fragmented and change over time, and King et al.'s prose generalization about default training is broader than its own table supports ([King et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1609/aies.v8i2.36646)). The repository linked to Wei et al. prints an API key and contains language/label inconsistencies; public code therefore improves auditability only when secrets, configurations, and paper–code consistency are checked ([official paper](https://doi.org/10.1609/icwsm.v20i1.42761); [repository](https://github.com/YuchengHE-CHN/ICWSM-Unpacking-Discourses-on-Childbirth-and-Parenthood)). Gustavsen et al. exclude a participant at reidentification risk while explicitly noting that this protection can silence a marginalized voice ([official paper](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/961193)).

Representational harm is not hypothetical. In firearm-violence interviews, safety refusals are triggered by graphic injury, sexual activity, race, AAE, and a slur used non-derogatorily; the authors report an average refusal rate of 44% and up to 65% of some participants' experience ignored ([Zhu et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.591/)). Japanese low-frequency and culturally specific content is unstable or absent ([Sakaguchi et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.2196/71521)). Ashwin et al. show that errors correlated with respondent characteristics can change policy-relevant estimates ([Ashwin et al., 2025/2026](https://doi.org/10.1177/00491241251338246)). In high-stakes research, unsupported clinical specificity, minority erasure, and majority-pattern dominance should be treated as safety outcomes, not merely “model limitations.”

Human review is necessary but must be specified. A qualified qualitative analyst should own the interpretation; a domain expert should assess clinical meaning; an evaluator independent of the reference creators should audit evidence; and participant or stakeholder validation should be considered for purposively sampled claims. Disagreement and negative cases should remain visible. “Full automation” is not defensible for clinical interpretation on the reviewed evidence: local small models can refuse or stereotype, frontier APIs raise privacy and drift concerns, and even high agreement can coexist with source errors or low-prevalence failure.

Synthetic participants are not an uncomplicated privacy substitute. Kapania et al. show that LLM-generated “participants” simulate stories without the situated agency, accountability, and lived experience that qualitative research seeks ([Kapania et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713220)). They can support stress tests or synthetic fixtures, but should not silently replace people whose experiences ground the research claim.

**RQ7 answer.** The reviewed reports variously document ethics approval, consent, de-identification, local or enterprise deployment, restricted access, provider assurances, and code/data release, but reporting of consent for model processing, retention/training terms, embedding risk, subgroup harm, credential hygiene, stakeholder authority, and downstream accountability is inconsistent. In high-stakes work, privacy, refusal, omission, stereotyping, unsupported specificity, and exclusion-induced silencing should be evaluated as outcomes, with a named human owner able to halt or reject the analysis.

# 13. Critical synthesis

## 13.1 Points of agreement

Across the inspected architectures, clear definitions, inclusion/exclusion criteria, relevant examples, and bounded tasks generally improve code application. Muse's error analysis attributes much disagreement to under-specified codebooks and inconsistent human annotation; its best ensemble reaches κ .703 only on well-specified codes and after per-code tuning ([Matveyenko et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-industry.131/)). Dai's iterative rationales and examples produce high human–machine agreement on small survey tasks ([Dai et al., 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.669/)). HICode and LLooM show that iterative abstraction scales better than one monolithic prompt ([Zhong et al., 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1580/); [Lam et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642830)). Evidence links and analyst-editable artifacts make outputs more inspectable and usable in these systems, even though they do not prove validity.

Ronaghi et al. sharpen the bounded-assistance conclusion in applied health services. Their model can organize researcher summaries and retrieve, verify, and sort quotation-grounded evidence across 167 interviews, making a deadline-constrained synthesis feasible; it cannot supply the actionable nuance, novelty judgment, contextual correction, or final intervention decisions, which remain human work ([Ronaghi et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14478)). NITA reaches a similar governance conclusion through a noncoding design, and Reflexis operationalizes optional disagreement and positionality support without replacing analyst authority ([NITA](https://doi.org/10.1080/14780887.2026.2638348); [Reflexis](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791275)).

The inspected evidence also repeatedly finds that output quality is conditional on prompt wording, role, chunking, model, threshold, codebook definition, context, and run. Zhu et al. run 118 experiments and find large quality changes from small processing/prompt differences; model size changes relevance only modestly ([Zhu et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.591/)). Muse finds eight of fifteen models outperform GPT-4o under its chosen prompt but recognizes that GPT-oriented optimization may favor nearby models ([Matveyenko et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-industry.131/)). TAMA's local Llama-3.1-8B evaluator approves poor themes early, while GPT-4o requires expert stopping because an automatic score plateaus and over-refinement appears ([Xu et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3828752)).

## 13.2 Points of conflict

**Reviewer synthesis among the inspected full texts:** the sharpest conflict is between high semantic/reference alignment and qualitative adequacy. Short-label studies report respectable BERTScore, but human experts prefer deeper alternatives and disagree substantially ([Parfenova et al., 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.361/)). Dörfel and Ammoneit likewise obtain 13/14 clear semantic code mappings and mean run stability of Jaccard .83, while coding-unit overlap with the human analysis is only .39 and domain-specific distinctions are lost ([Dörfel and Ammoneit, 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci16081314)). Clinical systems recover most reference themes, while audits find culturally specific omissions, dramatic rare-case overrepresentation, wrong source attribution, or narrative erasure ([Sakaguchi et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.2196/71521); [Raza et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01620); [Hill et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pdig.0001189); [Zhu et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.591/)). These are not contradictory facts: a system can match the central, majority reference concepts while disagreeing at the applied unit or mishandling the edges where qualitative insight and ethical stakes are greatest.

A second conflict concerns automation. Thematic-LM and Auto-TA frame autonomous end-to-end generation as scalable; DeTAILS, Dai, LLooM, TAMA, Muse, and CentaurTA retain varying degrees of analyst control ([Qiao et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3696410.3714595); [Yi et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23998); [DeTAILS](https://doi.org/10.1145/3719160.3735657); [Dai et al., 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.669/); [Lam et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642830); [Xu et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3828752); [Matveyenko et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-industry.131/); [Wang et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.778/)). The empirical evidence favors automation of bounded operations and human authority over interpretation. Role prompts labeled “qualitative researcher,” “surgeon,” or “layperson” do not create epistemic standpoint or accountability.

## 13.3 What apparent gaps are already partly closed

- **No-gold evaluation:** partly addressed by Chen's aggregate code space and Liu et al.'s blind, source-symmetric verification. Shared omissions, flooding, one-verifier dependence, theme-level warrants, and cross-domain construct validation remain ([Chen et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.2073/); [Liu et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28890)).
- **Provenance:** architecturally addressed by quote IDs, [clinical ledgers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08989), [MindCoder](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00775), [ROSAnnotator](https://doi.org/10.1109/HRI61500.2025.10974254), [Reflexis](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791275) histories, [Teleoscope](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791310) branches, and [ROCK](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/4q8bs_v2)'s versioned standard. Exactness, sufficiency, omission, participant spread, and independent-auditor usability are not jointly validated.
- **Human feedback and reflexivity:** addressed by [Dai](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.669/), [DeTAILS](https://doi.org/10.1145/3719160.3735657), [TAMA](https://doi.org/10.1145/3828752), [Muse](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-industry.131/), [CentaurTA](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.778/), [Co-Refine](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19309), [NITA](https://doi.org/10.1080/14780887.2026.2638348), [Nyaaba](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11850), [Reflexis](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791275), and the [K–12 instrument pipeline](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28889). Open questions concern anchoring, longitudinal outcome change, undetected error, burden, and whether deliberation improves independent interpretation rather than satisfaction.
- **Multi-agent analysis:** already crowded; novelty cannot rest on adding roles. Borchers' 5/432 versus 65/432 contrast directions make independent benefit especially doubtful. Agent identity, interaction, and advantage over strong sequential calls need ablation ([Borchers et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21039879)).
- **Fine-tuning and adaptation:** demonstrated by [Parfenova et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.361/), [Ganesh et al.](https://www.educationaldatamining.org/edm2024/proceedings/2024.EDM-posters.95/), [SFT-TA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17167), and other LoRA/steering studies; held-out concept/domain/model-family generalization and leakage-safe adaptation remain open.
- **Grounded-theory and noncoding workflows:** schema/hierarchy systems, [LOGOS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24294), [Neo-Grounded Theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25244), and [open–axial–selective instruments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28889) exist; [NITA](https://doi.org/10.1080/14780887.2026.2638348) demonstrates a deliberate noncoding alternative. Full theoretical sampling and validated theory development remain unestablished among inspected systems.
- **Stability and stopping:** repeated runs, no-new-theme rules, saturation ratios, fixed rounds, refinement trajectories, iterative-versus-batch comparisons, drift alerts, and analyst saturation confidence all exist ([Aggregate Code Space](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.2073/); [De Paoli–Mathis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04859); [QualiGPT](https://doi.org/10.54941/ahfe1006232); [Borchers et al.](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21039879); [Logan](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772363.3798789); [Reflexis](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791275); [Teleoscope](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791310)). Crossed seed×prompt×order×sample×model×analyst uncertainty and a held-out stopping rule tied to supported minority/negative-case gain and verification burden remain open.
- **Confidence/abstention:** rationale and confidence routes exist, but severe miscalibration, anchor copying, and weak steering make an uncalibrated confidence threshold unsafe ([Li et al., 2026/2027](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-29755-6_18)).
- **Multilingual and culturally situated work:** Finnish, Italian, German, Dutch, Japanese, translated East African, and East Asian platform studies exist ([Kousa et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2023.nlp4dh-1.12/); [Sakaguchi et al.](https://doi.org/10.2196/71521); [Mehta et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-18969-w); [Ashwin et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/00491241251338246)). Breadth, native-speaker evaluation, and cross-site/model transfer remain limited and uneven.

# 15. Recommended ARR research direction

The ranking is **reviewer judgment**, not a result of the quality-score totals. It weighs novelty after the inspected literature, ACL relevance, ethical importance, and seven-week feasibility. All three projects require a frozen protocol, exact model/configuration records, repeated runs, an explicit analytic contract, and source-grounded human evaluation.

## Rank 1 — VoiceTrace: participant- and source-aware validity for LLM thematic analysis

**Provisional title.** *Whose Voice Survives? Participant, Source, and Negative-Case Audits for LLM-Assisted Thematic Analysis*

**Central claim.** Conventional theme similarity can remain high while a pipeline relies on source/identity cues or loses, misattributes, stereotypes, and overamplifies participant evidence. A joint provenance-and-perturbation audit reveals these failures and changes system rankings or substantive conclusions.

**Research questions.** (1) How much code/theme variation is explained by participant, interviewer, site, identity condition, and context position? (2) Which participant-coverage, concentration, counterevidence, and source-warrant measures expose failures hidden by semantic overlap and LLM judging? (3) Which chunking, masking, retrieval, model-family, abstention, and human-review choices preserve rare and contradictory accounts?

**Novelty relative to inspected work.** Provenance, demographic sensitivity, and reflexivity are each occupied. [Subbiah](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20131) directly demonstrates demographic-conditioned narrative shifts; [Lukito](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-34570-7) shows interviewer confounding; [Parkington](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08002), [Bang](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocag123), [Mehta](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-18969-w), and [Gustavsen](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/961193) expose omitted or altered evidence; [MindCoder](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00775), [ROSAnnotator](https://doi.org/10.1109/HRI61500.2025.10974254), [clinical ledgers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08989), [Nyaaba](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11850), [Reflexis](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791275), [Teleoscope](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791310), and [ROCK](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/4q8bs_v2) provide lineage or deliberation components; [NITA](https://doi.org/10.1080/14780887.2026.2638348) preserves researcher authority. The defensible novelty is the **joint controlled validation** of exact source lineage, participant/source perturbations, voice and negative-case preservation, and independent warrants—not a new ledger or dashboard.

**Datasets required.** Two multi-speaker corpora with stable participant/source IDs and authorization for model processing, ideally one approved clinical or health-services corpus and one public nonclinical corpus; one site/domain fully held out. If fresh clinical authorization is not already complete, use an approved existing corpus plus a public set and a human-checked synthetic perturbation suite. Do not use AAOCA as the sole test family.

**Experimental design.** Freeze prompts before held-out evaluation. Cross at least one strong proprietary and one open-weight model with chunking, source masking, evidence retrieval, and three independent runs. Use participant/interviewer/site/domain-disjoint partitions; source-identity probes; removal of facilitator, boilerplate, diagnosis, and site cues; counterfactual identity swaps; rare/negative-case insert/remove/prevalence tests; context-position moves; and no-source-ID versus exact-source-ledger ablations. Report exact evidence precision, attribution error, participant recall/coverage/concentration, negative-case survival, unsupported-claim rate, conventional one/many-to-many similarity with threshold sensitivity, and at least one downstream conclusion change.

**Human evaluation required.** Separate reference creators from evaluators. Use at least two qualitative-method experts and two domain experts, blinded to condition and source, with independent judgments before adjudication. Stratify by participant prevalence, disagreement, negative-case status, source position, and model confidence. Ask whether the cited evidence supports the claim, what relevant evidence is omitted, and whether counterevidence changes the interpretation. Add purposive participant/stakeholder review for high-stakes claims if permitted.

**Ethics requirements.** Confirm consent and secondary-use scope for model processing; use local or approved enterprise deployment; document retention/training terms and embedding risk; suppress identifying quotations; preregister subgroup analyses; involve community/domain advisers in sentinel construction; and name the human who owns and can stop the interpretation.

**Key baselines.** Human-only analysis; single-pass/no-lineage generation; a codebook assignment baseline; HICode-style hierarchy; Thematic-LM/Auto-TA-style role pipeline; ROCK-compatible exact-source ledger; Reflexis-style deliberation; NITA-style researcher-primary condition; Subbiah-style demographic conditioning; no-source-ID ablation; and calibrated versus uncalibrated review routing.

**Expected contribution.** A benchmark and protocol, participant/source-aware metrics, controlled causal probes, and evidence that common rankings or conclusions change when voice preservation is measured. **Major risk:** unavailable multi-site data or insufficient expert time. **Mitigation:** freeze a two-corpus minimum, preregister the smallest defensible expert sample, release the perturbation/audit harness, and scope clinical claims to authorized data. **October feasibility:** high only if data and experts are secured immediately; otherwise medium with a nonclinical main experiment.

## Rank 2 — WarrantBench: plural, source-symmetric evaluation of codes and themes

**Provisional title.** *Beyond Agreement: Evaluating Competing Qualitative Interpretations Through Evidence Warrants*

**Central claim.** A multi-expert, source-grounded protocol that preserves alternative readings, counterevidence, and participant coverage can identify which automatic measures are useful under different analytic contracts without assuming one correct interpretation.

**Research questions.** (1) How do lexical, embedding, matching, plural-code-space, ICR, LLM-judge, blind-preference, provenance, and coverage measures relate to expert judgments at code and theme levels? (2) Do relationships change under coding-reliability, codebook, and reflexive contracts? (3) Which metric portfolio transfers to an unseen domain and model family while detecting unsupported or unrepresentative interpretations?

**Novelty relative to inspected work.** [Aggregate Code Space](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.2073/), [source-symmetric Bradley–Terry comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28890), [IDEAlign](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02855) perturbations, [Simon's blinded comparison](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-98420-4_5), [specialist/lay clinical ratings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19008), [ICR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04413), [Han's LLM-judge calibration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00008), [Misra's context/theme-fit audit](https://doi.org/10.1177/16094069261426100), and [Logan's iterative/batch comparison](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772363.3798789) already cover parts of the problem. Novelty must be **theme-level, multi-expert, cross-contract, source-grounded construct validation on held-out domains/models**, not another scalar metric or “agreement is not quality” slogan.

**Datasets required.** Three domains with source IDs and at least two genuinely independent human interpretations per domain; a minimum viable version can use two deep domains plus a held-out transfer set. Include different qualitative contracts and purposively sampled minority/negative cases.

**Experimental design.** Generate outputs from two model families and two workflows. Build balanced code/theme comparisons covering paraphrase, split/merge, contradiction, generic abstraction, unsupported novelty, shared omission, source misattribution, and minority evidence. Experts blind to source judge central claim, evidence sufficiency, counterevidence, participant spread, alternative defensibility, and contract fitness. Fit hierarchical/generalizability models with evaluator/domain effects; hold out one domain and one model family; publish calibration, intervals, and error strata rather than one leaderboard.

**Human evaluation required.** At least three qualified experts per domain or a crossed incomplete-block design supporting reliability and generalizability estimates. Collect rationales and positionality information, retain individual disagreement before adjudication, randomize order, and disclose sampling and multiplicity.

**Ethics requirements.** Fair compensation; consent-compatible source quotation use; de-identification and access control; no unnecessary source-identity exposure; respectful disagreement-preserving release; and stakeholder input on harms and rubric language in sensitive domains.

**Key baselines.** ROUGE/METEOR; BERTScore/cosine; one-to-one and many-to-many matching; Aggregate Code Space dimensions; IDEAlign; ICR; repeated-run similarity; same- and cross-family LLM judges; source-symmetric Bradley–Terry; human-oracle/reference creators; quote exactness and participant coverage. No LLM judge may be the sole verifier.

**Expected contribution.** An empirical construct map showing which metrics predict independently warranted interpretation under which contract, plus a reusable judgment set. **Major risk:** annotation burden and the objection that judgments remain situated. **Mitigation:** preregister constructs, use a powered incomplete-block design, preserve rationales/disagreement, and test generalization rather than claiming a universal gold metric. **October feasibility:** medium; two rigorous domains are preferable to three shallow ones.

## Rank 3 — StopTA: held-out evidence-based stopping for iterative refinement

**Provisional title.** *When Should an Agent Stop Refining? Evidence-Gain Criteria for Iterative Thematic Analysis*

**Central claim.** Fixed rounds, no-new-theme rules, similarity plateaus, confidence, and judge scores do not reliably identify improvement. A frozen rule balancing marginal supported participant/negative-case evidence against redundancy, unsupported claims, instability, and verification burden transfers better across models and domains.

**Research questions.** (1) What changes over refinement trajectories at evidence, participant, theme, and conclusion levels? (2) Which stopping rule transfers across models, domains, and pipelines? (3) Does exposing a stopping signal help analysts avoid over-refinement without anchoring or hiding minority cases?

**Novelty relative to inspected work.** [CAILA](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3995/LLMQUAL_paper4.pdf) implements no-new-theme stopping; [De Paoli–Mathis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04859) estimate saturation; [TALLMesh](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13892) allocates transcript batches; [LOGOS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24294) and [LLMCode](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16671) retain refinement trajectories; [TAMA](https://doi.org/10.1145/3828752) shows plateau and expert stopping; [clinical refinement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08989) runs up to ten rounds; [CentaurTA](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.778/) can degrade; [Teleoscope](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791310) captures analyst saturation confidence; [Logan](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772363.3798789) directly compares iterative and batch modes. The open claim is transferable held-out stopping with a joint evidential objective, not invention of a stopping signal.

**Datasets required.** Three existing public or approved corpora with stable source IDs, including at least one with rare/negative cases and one held-out domain. Save every intermediate artifact and human revision.

**Experimental design.** Produce complete ten-round trajectories for at least two models and two pipelines. Learn or calibrate only on development corpora, freeze the rule, and test on the third. Evaluate marginal supported evidence, participant/negative-case coverage, redundancy, unsupported claims, substantive instability, and verification time. Report Pareto fronts and component ablations; never select the best round on test material.

**Human evaluation required.** Blinded experts compare adjacent iterations and stopped outputs for supported improvement, lost nuance, counterevidence, minority preservation, and verification burden. A small analyst study can test whether a signal changes stopping behavior and whether it anchors users.

**Ethics requirements.** Apply the same source/privacy controls as Rank 1; recognize that intermediate artifacts can retain sensitive quotations; minimize retention; and make the analyst—not the stopping score—the final authority.

**Key baselines.** Fixed 1/3/5/10 rounds; no-new-theme; saturation ratio; TALLMesh allocation; iterative versus batch; same- and cross-family judge thresholds; reference-similarity plateau; human stop; Teleoscope-style saturation confidence; confidence/abstention; and Pareto-front reporting.

**Expected contribution.** Public stopping trajectories, a frozen rule, and evidence that “more refinement” is not monotonic and that the chosen stopping point transfers. **Major risk:** a hand-weighted composite that overfits. **Mitigation:** preregister components, fit no weights on test data, compare Pareto alternatives, and validate with independent human trajectories. **October feasibility:** high computationally and medium for the required expert study.

## Recommendation

Choose Rank 1 if participant/source identifiers, processing authorization, and evaluator access are already secured. **Reviewer judgment among the inspected full texts:** it offers the strongest combination of novelty, ACL relevance, and ethical consequence because it joins mechanisms that existing studies examine separately. Choose Rank 3 if those prerequisites are unavailable within days; it is more feasible but must demonstrate held-out transfer. Rank 2 is potentially the most foundational, but the existing no-gold and judge-calibration literature makes a rushed single-domain or single-expert version unlikely to clear the novelty bar.

# 16. Limitations of the review

This is a single-reviewer, Codex-assisted scoping review, not an independently duplicated screen or extraction. All 1,360 deduplicated database records now have terminal title/abstract dispositions, but terminal does not mean error-free: the initial 122/612/626 high/uncertain/low triage was only a prioritization device, and the later eligibility decisions were not independently adjudicated. Parallel extraction, schema assertions, DOI/title deduplication, evidence locations, and record-specific reasons improve auditability but do not create inter-reviewer agreement.

Retrieval was incomplete. The primary unique-identity flow sought 317 reports, did not retrieve 53, and assessed or resolved 264; the latter produced 237 retained reports, 18 full-text exclusions, and nine publication/version-family resolutions. No methodological or result claim in this synthesis derives from a nonretrieved report or publisher abstract. The appendix gives every nonretrieval its recorded retrieval disposition and every full-text exclusion its record-specific reason; four nonretrieval source records lack a specific reason and are labeled only as not retrieved after documented attempts rather than being supplemented by inference. Because plausible full texts remain inaccessible, all novelty and absence claims are bounded to reviewer judgment among the inspected full texts.

Coverage also limits recall. PubMed and arXiv supplied reproducible exports, but Crossref searches were relevance-ranked and capped, the arXiv identity query was capped, ACL retrieval was title-oriented, and OpenReview candidates were client-filtered. Stable auditable bulk exports were unavailable for Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, ACM DL, IEEE Xplore, Web of Science, and Scopus in the available environment. Citation chaining preserved parent-seed provenance but was focused and index-dependent; no exhaustive saturation claim is made.

Publication status near the cutoff is unusually fluid. Preprint, online-first, acceptance, event, and formal-proceedings years can differ. Auto-TA is arXiv:2506.23998v2 with OpenReview label `ACL-SRW 2025 Poster`; no archival ACL Anthology paper was verified ([preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23998); [OpenReview history](https://openreview.net/forum?id=vWVXGfwONH)). SFT-TA is arXiv:2509.17167v1 with OpenReview label `ML4H 2025 RejectfromProceedingsasFindingsPoster`; its `PMLR XXX` text is not an archival citation ([preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17167); [OpenReview history](https://openreview.net/forum?id=vvdcTphibg)). The retained AIED confidence paper was available as a 2026 conference manuscript but has a formal Springer 2027 proceedings citation and is labeled accordingly ([Li et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-29755-6_18)). The ATA-LLM paper was associated with the SEET 2025 event and published in 2026 ([Beltrán et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-08977-9_29)). Where peer review or acceptance was not established by 24 August 2026, the bibliography says so rather than inferring status from a DOI or template.

The corpus is heterogeneous and dependence-rich, so no meta-analysis was attempted. Identically named metrics use different formulas; coding units and denominators differ; studies reuse datasets, human themes, prompts, or preprint/VOR families; and private data prevent independent source verification. Quality scores are reviewer judgments reported by dimension, not a total-score ranking. The 34-record method-congruence audit uses an explicit strict lower-bound rubric, but other qualified reviewers could reasonably code ambiguous traditions differently.

Model evidence is time-dependent. Proprietary snapshots, hidden serving changes, prompts, chunking, retries, seeds, and evaluator configurations are frequently incomplete. Citation counts are dynamic and retained only with their source and retrieval date in the master. Finally, the ARR recommendation depends on local facts the literature cannot provide—data authorization, compute, evaluator availability, stakeholder access, and engineering readiness—so its feasibility ranking must be revised if those prerequisites differ.

# 17. Conclusion

LLMs now assist nearly every visible artifact of qualitative work: familiarization proxies, quotation retrieval, open and deductive codes, codebooks, hierarchies, noncoding themes, memos and schemas, reports, disagreement/confidence triage, stopping signals, evidence links, version histories, and standards. **Reviewer synthesis among the inspected full texts:** the most defensible systems keep these artifacts editable and traceable, preserve analyst authority and disagreement, declare the analytic contract, calibrate uncertainty, and audit sources and participants with independent human judgment.

The evidence is strongest for well-specified code application, retrieval, and scalable organization. It is weaker—not wholly absent—for reflexive, culturally situated, minority-sensitive, theory-building, and clinically consequential interpretation. New direct evidence makes the boundary clearer: high semantic similarity, run agreement, topic coherence, or verbal confidence can coexist with low coding-unit overlap, quote alteration, systematic overcoding, rare/implicit-theme loss, demographic distortion, and interviewer confounding. A source link improves auditability but does not prove that an interpretation is exact, sufficient, representative, or warranted.

**RQ8 answer.** The open research opportunity is no longer “generate themes with agents,” “add provenance,” “prompt reflexivity,” “repeat runs,” or “stop when no new themes appear.” Those components exist. The strongest defensible ARR direction is a joint participant/source-aware validity audit that controls identity and context while measuring exact evidence, omission, concentration, negative-case survival, independent warrants, and changed conclusions. Multi-domain multi-expert warrant validation and held-out evidence-based stopping remain credible alternatives; calibrated abstention is an important component, not a substitute for either. These are reviewer judgments bounded to 24 August 2026, the inspected full texts, and immediate feasibility constraints.

## 19.4 Core-paper quality rubric and audit

Exactly **181 quality rows** correspond one-to-one with the 181 core evidence IDs. No noncore record is scored. Each dimension contains both a 0/1/2 score and a paper-specific rationale. Scores are interpreted against the paper’s stated claim: 0=absent/inadequate, 1=partial/unclear, 2=adequate. Totals are navigation aids and must not be used to declare a universally “best” study.

| Dimension | What the reviewer assessed |
|---|---|
| Clarity of its qualitative methodology | Whether the claimed tradition, analytic contract, unit, and operational steps are explicit and methodologically congruent. |
| Appropriateness of its dataset | Whether the data, sampling, size, context, language, and sensitivity can support the stated claim. |
| Transparency of its computational workflow | Whether model/version, prompts, chunking/context, adaptation, agents, retries, and intervention points are reproducible. |
| Strength and independence of its baselines | Whether comparators are strong, independent, leakage-safe, and evaluated on equivalent units/tasks. |
| Adequacy of its human evaluation | Whether evaluator expertise/count, independence, blinding, sampling, rubric, disagreement, and uncertainty support the claim. |
| Validity of its automatic metrics | Whether the metric operationalizes the intended construct, is human-validated, and reports units, thresholds, uncertainty, and statistics. |
| Treatment of interpretive plurality | Whether alternative readings, positionality, disagreement, and limits of a single reference are acknowledged or preserved. |
| Evidence grounding and traceability | Whether source-to-output links preserve exact text, speaker/context, versions, warrants, and auditable omissions. |
| Stability and reproducibility testing | Whether repeated runs or crossed perturbations test substantive—not merely lexical—stability and resources permit rerun. |
| Ethics, privacy, and responsible-research reporting | Whether consent, ethics, deployment, retention/training, privacy, representation, stakeholder authority, and accountability are addressed. |
| Availability of data, prompts, code, and model versions | Whether usable data or fixtures, prompts/configuration, code, and exact model/version information are available. |
| Whether conclusions are proportionate to the evidence | Whether claims remain within the evaluated task, data, method, status, denominators, and acknowledged limitations. |

The claimed-versus-operationalized audit in Section 4.1 covers all 181 core records: 169 have both substantive fields, ten have neither, one is claim-only, and one operation-only. Under the stated strict lower-bound rubric, 34 receive a material mismatch/narrowing flag; those flags are reviewer judgments separate from quality totals.
