Human-written summary: The interviewee describes the chapters of her life story. She titles the irst chapter, “The Formative Years”, and describes her early childhood. She feels she had a
“very happy childhood”, and in particular remembers holiday celebrations feeling like they lasted many weeks. She remembers learning about sexual intercourse from her friends, and feeling
unnerved and disbelieving, especially in contrast with her education at the time in a Catholic school. Overall, she felt she had a middle-class or lower middle-class childhood where she had
everything she needed, not everything she wanted, and that gave her structure in her life. Next, she moves onto a chapter she calls, “The High School Years." She was initially upset because all her
friends were going to a different school, but her mother wanted her to attend a nearby Catholic girls school. She ended up having a great experience, appreciating the single-gender environment,
and leaving with “pride in who [she] was." In school, she was “very, very good” at shorthand and “it came easily to [her]." The next chapter she labels, “Finding Myself." She had seen her parents
struggle inancially growing up and she and her siblings had tried to help out. She had two brothers, but they both had severe health issues. Given these factors, she initially decided to become an
administrative assistant to prioritize making money. She found it jarring to enter the working world and during the training for her irst position, she felt so overwhelmed that she didn’t take the
job. This overwhelm ate away at her but eventually she realized, “you just take one day at a time, kind of like baby steps”, and she was able to take a position at another company and advance her
career. She moves next into the chapter of “Motherhood”, which she describes as the highlight of her life. She met her husband through work and they dated for several years, broke up at one
point and eventually got back together and got engaged. She left her job when she became pregnant with her irst child. She says she left her job because “at that time,… you basically stayed
home and raised your kids." She then continues to the next chapter of “Years I Was Raising my Children” – she had three children who are all now adults. They are the “light of [her] life,” and she
feels “there's nothing more important than you can do in your life” than raising your children. Now that her children are adults, she feels it’s her turn in life and she wants to “spread [her] wings
and ly a little bit more." While raising her children though, she felt she had the “luxury of being able to stay home with them” because her husband worked two full-time jobs. She describes
herself as happily married and inserts a chapter on her husband, crying through most of her description of him. She says “he’s always been there for [her]” and is “such a good guy." Finally, she
ends with the last chapter: “The Awakening." She feels excited about “so many new things” happening for her and is enjoying being a grandmother. She is re lecting on her own mother, who is the
“big boss” and “has aged beautifully." She hopes to be an inspiration for her children and grandchildren in the same way her mother has been for her.

A   Interview Questions


To parse interviews automatically into which sections correspond to the answers to these questions, we
first identify interviewer utterances by the "INTERVIEWER:" tag. We then encode these utterances
using a TF-IDF vectorization. We encode the text of each interview question with the same method, and
then for each question, we retrieve the interviewer utterance with the highest cosine similarity match. If
the initial cosine similarity is > 0.7, we consider this a match, and if the similarity is < 0.3, we consider
the question skipped in the interview. Then, using the matched questions as anchors, we search only
the text between neighboring anchored questions for the remaining questions, and iteratively lower the
cosine similarity threshold by 0.1 until we reach the floor of 0.3. For example, if questions 2 and 4 were
already found, then we would search for question 3 only in the utterances between these two questions.
The full code will be released in the accompanying GitHub Repository.
