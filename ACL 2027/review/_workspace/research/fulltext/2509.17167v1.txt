surface-level form and is especially valuable when
comparing paraphrased or reworded themes that dif-
fer lexically but align semantically (e.g., “navigat-
ing complex medical decisions” vs. “making difficult
treatment choices”, or “emotional response to diag-
nosis” vs. “coping with the news”).
   BLEU (Papineni et al., 2002) computes the pre-
cision of overlapping n-grams, applying a brevity
penalty to discourage short outputs. It tends to
under-reward paraphrases or semantically aligned
variants due to its strict reliance on exact matches.
For example, “managing stress and anxiety” vs.
“handling emotional pressure” would receive a low
BLEU score despite similar meaning.
   METEOR (Banerjee and Lavie, 2005) addresses
BLEU’s limitations by incorporating unigram recall,
stemming, synonym matching (via WordNet), and a
fragmentation penalty. It can give partial credit to
pairs like “healthcare access barriers” and “difficulty
reaching medical services” even without exact word
matches.
   ROUGE-L (Lin, 2004) measures the longest com-
mon subsequence between reference and candidate
texts, capturing both content and order overlap. For
instance, “struggles after diagnosis” and “challenges
following diagnosis” would yield moderate ROUGE-
L scores due to shared sequential structure.


Appendix G. Human Evaluation
            Guiding Statements
For the human evaluation, raters were instructed to
assess themes on a 5-point Likert scale (1 = strongly
disagree, 5 = strongly agree) according to the follow-
ing guiding statements:

  • Coverage: These themes comprehensively cap-
    ture parents’ lived experiences and are supported
    by multiple participants.

  • Actionability: This theme provides clear, spe-
    cific insights that can inform interventions, re-
    sources, or research.

  • Distinctiveness: This theme is clearly distinct
    from other themes, with no major overlaps or
    redundancies.

  • Relevance: This theme accurately reflects par-
    ents’ lived experiences and is directly related to
    having a child with AAOCA.
