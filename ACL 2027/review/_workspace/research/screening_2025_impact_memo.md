## Material changes to RQ1–RQ8

| RQ | Report-level change required from the inspected 2025 evidence |
|---|---|
| **RQ1—tasks** | The task map is substantially broader: open coding, deductive assignment, quotation selection, codebook induction/reduction, hierarchical grouping, theme generation/refinement, noncoding theme development, report generation, evidence retrieval, automated verification, confidence-based triage, and stopping all have implemented examples. NITA is a direct counterexample to any claim that all systems operationalize themes through coding. LOGOS and Neo-Grounded Theory claim theory/schema development, but their evidence supports automated schema optimization more clearly than grounded-theory development. Conceptual-model/theory claims should therefore be described as implemented but not substantiated. |
| **RQ2—methodological grounding** | Claimed methodology and computation must be counted separately. Reviewer coding identified **at least 15 material mismatches or narrowings among the 61 core reports** (a judgmental audit count, not a prevalence estimate): Hairston (claimed inductive TA, evaluated fixed-theme classification), Shah (calls zero-shot fixed multiclass labeling “inductive”), Mackenzie (legal-field extraction called thematic analysis), Osian (only the relevance screen is validated), Ghali (embedding clusters named as themes), Sulaiman (factor reduction used as theme construction), Koh (fixed complaint fields plus an unvalidated generated topic set), Parkington (reflexive-TA label with a hierarchical codebook/reliability comparison), HHitL-TA (reflexive-TA label with theory-bound codebook, IRR, magnitude scores, and latent classes), LOGOS and Neo-Grounded Theory (automated schema optimization presented as grounded theory), Shen (binary sentence classification), Borchers (eight-code classification called thematic coding), Rodriguez (17-dimension document classification), and Relins (four-label narrative classification). The report should never pool these with open-ended theme interpretation as one performance task. |
| **RQ3—architecture** | The new corpus adds confidence–diversity routing, self/cross verification, prompt- and model-ensembles, theory-context ablations, sliding windows, RAG/knowledge-base prompting, same-model deliberation, multi-model deliberation, graph refinement, human-editable provenance chains, and repeated inference. Architecture labels remain unreliable: multiple “multi-agent” systems use identical-model instances, and consensus is therefore not independent triangulation. Exact snapshot, run/seed, chunking, order, and context disclosure is still commonly incomplete. |
| **RQ4—human role** | Human involvement ranges from post-hoc checking to primary interpretive authority. Mackenzie calls its workflow human-in-the-loop although humans principally create/evaluate reference annotations; Qiao asks humans to accept AI-proposed codes, creating anchoring risk; LLMCode lets analysts revise their own baseline after seeing model output; HHitL-TA logs accept/merge/reject decisions; NITA keeps humans responsible for positionality, counterevidence, theme retention, and the final meta-narrative. The report should classify implemented intervention points, not repeat “collaborative” or “human-in-the-loop” labels. |
| **RQ5—evaluation** | Several new results materially strengthen the conclusion that agreement/stability is not validity. Parkington finds both LLM workflows miss 190/428 human excerpts; the knowledge-base condition overlaps only 124/428 human excerpts versus 238/428 out-of-the-box and substitutes interpretive summaries for 68/251 purported quotations. QualiGPT reports human–human κ=.73, human-inclusive GPT-4 κ about .46, and GPT-4 self-agreement κ=.87; newer GPT-4o/Claude comparisons are .38/.42. IDEAlign's best expert-aligned correlations are .618/.683 versus human-oracle .906/.812 and shows style edits can inflate embedding similarity by up to 82%. Borchers finds only 5/432 contrasts favor multi-agent consensus and 65 favor single agents. Than finds GPT only .01–.03 above the best open model in most fixed-coding comparisons. These results require construct-specific reporting: source coverage, quote exactness, rare-code behavior, and expert warrants alongside κ/F1/cosine/Jaccard. |
| **RQ6—validity threats** | Newly demonstrated threats include development-set reuse (Flanders, LLMCode), label leakage (Koh), prevalence-enriched evaluation (Osian), severe class imbalance (Hairston, Than, Li), same-family LLM judging (Castellanos, Neo-Grounded Theory), context/preprocessing drift (Na/Feng), human-baseline drift/anchoring (LLMCode), style sensitivity (IDEAlign), keyword and subgroup shortcuts (Rizvi; Sakib), platform/language/time confounding and possible minority-cluster removal (Wei), and omission of emotional/domain-specific detail (Parkington; Gustavsen; Shanwetter et al.). The Borchers paper's reported 77,273 decisions also fails to reconcile with the stated full factorial (500,256), so denominators must be audited rather than copied. |
| **RQ7—ethics** | Ethical reporting remains uneven even in sensitive applications. Balt uses a local Llama model with ethics approval/consent; Abramson describes restricted-data infrastructure and avoids commercial transfer. By contrast, Mehta sends translated sensitive focus-group data through an API/Colab workflow without demonstrating the breadth of its provider assurance; the oncology comparison reports IRB/consent but not consent or retention terms for proprietary transfer; Gustavsen excludes one reidentification-risk participant and explicitly notes that exclusion can silence a marginalized voice. King’s privacy-policy analysis shows policies are fragmented, mutable, and difficult to reproduce, but its blanket prose claim about default training is broader than its own table. Kapania adds direct empirical constraints on substituting synthetic “participants” for situated people. |
| **RQ8—open opportunities** | The evidence narrows, but does not close, the three proposed directions. The strongest surviving opportunity is a participant/source-aware benchmark that joins exact evidence provenance, minority/negative-case preservation, controlled source/identity perturbations, and independent expert warrants. A generic multi-agent system, semantic-overlap metric, quote ledger, repeated-run analysis, or no-new-theme stopping rule is no longer novel by itself. Absence claims must be scoped to inspected full texts, especially because six plausible-core reports remain unretrieved. |

## Gap-claim corrections

The report should make the following replacements during synthesis:

1. Replace “multi-agent analysis is missing” with “multi-agent architectures are crowded, but independent benefit is unproven.” Borchers shows minimal or negative consensus effects; Parfenova shows convergence can be semantic flattening; Simon shows two blinded experts can reach sharply different comparative judgments.
2. Replace “stability testing is absent” with “repeated-run testing exists but rarely crosses seed × prompt × order × sample × model and rarely validates substantive stability.” Jain’s high within-model κ/cosine on one transcript has no human baseline; De Paoli–Mathis’s stable duplicate-rate saturation statistic lacks human semantic validation.
3. Replace “provenance is absent” with “source links and editable ledgers exist, but their exactness, sufficiency, representativeness, omission detection, and auditor usability are seldom jointly validated.” MindCoder, ROSAnnotator, Osian, HHitL-TA, De Paoli, and NITA provide partial implementations.
4. Replace “human-in-the-loop workflows are absent” with “human review is common but often post hoc, incompletely logged, or vulnerable to anchoring; prospective decision value remains weakly tested.”
5. Replace “non-English or cross-cultural work is absent” with “it is limited and methodologically uneven.” The inspected evidence includes Japanese interviews, Kenyan translated focus groups, and China/Japan/Korea social-media analysis; the Korean inductive-coding paper TA-0577 remains abstract-only and cannot support methods claims.
6. Replace “grounded-theory automation is absent” with “several systems claim it, but operationalization and theory-development validity are contested” (Yue, LOGOS, Neo-Grounded Theory).
7. Preserve the open claim on participant/minority/negative-case auditing, but qualify it as reviewer judgment among inspected full texts. No inspected study jointly measures participant-level evidence recall/precision, concentration, contradiction survival, source-identity shortcuts, and unsupported synthesis under controlled perturbations.

## Impact on the three ARR projects

The additional evidence does **not** justify reversing the existing ranking, but it changes the novelty boundary for every project.

1. **VoiceTrace remains Rank 1 and is strengthened.** Parkington's 190/428 missed human excerpts, Mehta's altered/truncated/composite quotations, Gustavsen's loss of emotion/context/domain distinctions, Rizvi and Sakib's shortcut/subgroup failures, and Wei's cross-platform/minority-removal risks directly motivate participant- and source-aware evaluation. The contribution must combine speaker/source-disjoint or counterfactual probes with evidence precision/recall, participant coverage/concentration, negative-case survival, and blinded expert warrant review. A provenance interface alone is insufficient.
2. **WarrantBench remains Rank 2 but faces a higher adjacency/novelty bar.** IDEAlign already supplies expert triplet judgments, perturbation tests, sample-size stability, and stronger alignment than embeddings; Simon already conducts blinded human/AI code/theme judgments; Moskalewicz uses internal experts, two blinded specialists, and 115 lay raters. The defensible contribution must therefore be source-grounded, theme-level, multi-expert, plurality-preserving, crossed across analytic contracts/domains, and validated on a held-out model/domain—not merely pairwise preference or “agreement is not quality.”
3. **StopTA remains Rank 3, with its simple forms partly closed.** CAILA already stops after no new themes, De Paoli–Mathis proposes an inductive-thematic-saturation ratio, TALLMesh forces unassigned-code allocation, LOGOS traces ten refinements and can decline in adequacy/consistency, and LLMCode studies iterative alignment. Novelty requires a frozen, held-out stopping rule that balances newly supported participant/negative-case coverage against redundancy, unsupported claims, instability, and human verification burden, with transfer across systems/domains.

Quality totals in the companion are diagnostic aids, not rankings. The strongest design evidence for the ARR argument comes from converging failure mechanisms and task-matched evaluations, not from sorting papers by a summed score.
