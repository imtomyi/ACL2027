Executive synthesis
This review maps 237 unique, full-text-grounded evidence records: 181 core reports with one 12-
dimension quality assessment each and 56 adjacent, contextual, or methodological records retained to
interpret validity, ethics, and qualitative-method claims. The field has moved rapidly from asking a chat
model to suggest codes to constructing hierarchies, applying codebooks at scale, orchestrating role-
prompted pipelines, learning persistent instructions from analyst feedback, supporting noncoding
theme development, and retaining versioned source lineage. Dai, Xiong, and Ku made codebook
revision, rationale, and stopping human decisions (Dai et al., 2023); LLooM organized concept induction
as an analyst-editable loop (Lam et al., 2024); HICode and Thematic-LM scaled hierarchical or role-
prompted induction (Zhong et al., 2025; Qiao et al., 2025). By the cutoff, NITA kept positionality,
counterevidence, theme retention, and the final meta-narrative with human researchers without
making coding mandatory; Reflexis logged code evolution and surfaced drift, disagreement, and
positionality prompts; and ROCK specified machine-readable source spans, codebooks, relations, and
versions (Nguyen, Trung, and Nguyen, 2026; Ye et al., 2026; Peters et al., 2026).

What is technically possible remains narrower than “automated thematic analysis.” Models can propose
manifest codes, apply defined deductive labels, cluster and name concepts, retrieve candidate
quotations, draft themes and reports, compare runs, and expose intermediate artifacts for correction.
They do not thereby supply researcher positionality, theoretical sensitivity, cultural accountability, or an
independent warrant for interpretation. Even apparently strong code application is conditional. Muse
reports κ=.703 for an ensemble on 12 well-specified codes after per-code threshold tuning, whereas Hill
et al. report high percentage agreement but κ=.34 in a low-prevalence health task ( Matveyenko et al.,
2026; Hill et al., 2026). Dörfel and Ammoneit found 13/14 semantic code mappings above .60 and mean
run Jaccard .83, yet coding-unit overlap with the human analysis was .39 and domain-specific
distinctions were lost (Dörfel and Ammoneit, 2026).

The principal weakness is construct validity, not a shortage of metrics. Parkington et al.'s two LLM
workflows each missed 190/428 human excerpts; their out-of-box and knowledge-base conditions
overlapped only 238/428 and 124/428 excerpts, and 68/251 purported knowledge-base quotations
were interpretive summaries rather than quotations (Parkington et al., 2025). QualiGPT's GPT self-
agreement (κ=.87) exceeded its human-inclusive agreement (about .46), despite human–human κ=.73
(Zhang et al., 2025). IDEAlign's best automated correlations with expert judgments were .618/.683
versus a human oracle's .906/.812; style-only edits inflated embedding similarity by as much as 82%
(Nam et al., 2025). ICR found human reliability of .86–1.00 and LLM reliability of .35–.76 even when
cosine and F1 were high (Pérez et al., 2026). These results do not invalidate similarity or reliability
measures; they show why each operation must be validated against its intended qualitative construct.

No-gold evaluation, provenance, reflexivity interfaces, multi-agent consensus, and elementary stopping
rules are no longer empty spaces. Aggregate Code Space diagnostics preserve multiple code sets but can
reward code flooding and cannot recover concepts omitted by every coder (Chen et al., 2026). Blind
source-symmetric verification shows that lower human–LLM agreement need not imply lower expert
preference, but it uses one verifier, one codebook, and code assignment rather than theme
interpretation (Liu et al., 2026). Reflexis and Teleoscope implement histories, branching, and
deliberation; clinical and production pipelines maintain quote or turn identifiers; CAILA, saturation-ratio
studies, LOGOS trajectories, and iterative-versus-batch comparisons supply stopping-related signals (Ye
et al., 2026; Lee et al., 2026; Lin et al., 2025; De Paoli and Mathis, 2025; Pi et al., 2025). Reviewer
judgment among the inspected full texts: what remains open is independent, cross-domain validation
of exactness, omission, participant spread, rare and negative cases, disagreement, and auditor usability.

Culturally and clinically situated interpretation reveals bidirectional failure. Japanese clinical work
recovered common descriptive themes more readily than low-frequency or culturally specific ones;
“fate” was absent (Sakaguchi et al., 2025). Open models analyzing interviews with Black male firearm-
violence survivors produced thousands of labels, averaged about 10% relevance, refused an average
44% of prompts, and erased trauma, sexual-health, racial, and African American English content even
when they recovered formal human codes before aggregation (Zhu et al., 2026). Conversely, LLM-TA
overemphasized a rare dramatic event (Raza et al., 2025). Subbiah et al.'s three-model, five-seed
analysis of 154 longitudinal interviews found demographic-conditioned semantic, affective, and
thematic shifts; experts reported stereotyping and omission of deaths, violence, abuse, and addiction
(Subbiah et al., 2026). Lukito et al. further showed that topic models missed masking, self-doubt, and
strengths and that apparent diagnosis clustering was confounded by interviewer identity (Lukito et al.,
2026). Minority preservation is therefore not a one-directional recall problem: omission, dilution,
misattribution, stereotyping, refusal, and sensational overrepresentation all require measurement.

Reviewer judgment among the inspected full texts: the strongest October opportunity remains a
plurality- and provenance-aware audit of whose voice survives aggregation, but its novelty is the joint
validation, not a new ledger or interface. It should retain exact speaker-to-quotation-to-code-to-theme
paths; cross participant, interviewer, site, source, and identity perturbations; quantify participant
concentration and negative-case survival; and use independent qualitative and domain experts to judge
evidence warrants and omissions. VoiceTrace remains Rank 1, WarrantBench Rank 2, and StopTA Rank
3, conditional on immediate access to authorized data and evaluators.

1. Introduction and motivation
Qualitative analysis turns situated language into claims about experience, practice, meaning, and social
structure. Its labor is not merely labeling: analysts read and reread a corpus, memo, compare cases,
revise concepts, inspect contradiction, locate counterevidence, and write an interpretation whose
warrant should remain visible in the data. Fluent codes and plausible themes are easy for an LLM to
generate; establishing methodological congruence, evidential sufficiency, representativeness, reflexivity,
and safety is much harder.

The reviewed literature spans different tasks and epistemologies. Some papers predict a short label for a
presegmented quotation; others apply a fixed codebook, induce a hierarchy, create themes, build an
interactive workbench, support noncoding narrative synthesis, or evaluate competing code spaces.
Treating all of these as one “thematic analysis” benchmark erases the claim being tested. Parfenova et
al. evaluate one-code-per-segment generation rather than axial or theme construction (Parfenova et al.,
2025); QuaLLM combines extraction, classification, aggregation, and prevalence estimation (Rao et al.,
2025); NITA treats coding as optional and keeps interpretive synthesis with researchers (Nguyen, Trung,
and Nguyen, 2026). A fixed-label F1 or κ claim can be sound without licensing a claim of equivalent
reflexive, grounded-theory, or clinical interpretation.

This review therefore asks both what systems do and what their evidence licenses us to conclude. It
covers 181 core computational or empirical reports and 56 adjacent, contextual, and methodological
records through 24 August 2026. Preprints, accepted manuscripts, posters, protocols, and position
papers are retained where they define the frontier, but are labeled separately from verified peer-
reviewed work. Publication families are counted once at the preferred verified version, with event-year,
online-first, preprint, and formal-proceedings histories preserved where they differ.

2. Review questions
The review addresses eight questions.

   1. RQ1—tasks and methods: Which parts of familiarization, quotation selection, open or deductive
      coding, codebook induction/refinement, axial or hierarchical coding, noncoding theme
      development, subtheme/theme creation, evidence retrieval, memo/schema construction,
      conceptual modeling, reporting, confidence/abstention, stopping, and quality assurance are
      automated or assisted?

   2. RQ2—methodological grounding: Does each study claim and operationalize reflexive TA,
      codebook TA, coding-reliability TA, grounded theory, qualitative content analysis, Framework
      analysis, rapid analysis, noncoding qualitative synthesis, generic coding, or topic discovery? Where
      does the implemented operation narrow or conflict with the label?

   3. RQ3—architecture: Which model versions, prompt/adaptation strategies, agents or ensembles,
      retrieval and graph components, chunking/context strategies, stochastic controls,
      confidence/diversity routes, evidence links, stopping rules, and intervention points form the
      workflow?

   4. RQ4—human–AI relationship: Is the human absent after setup, a question/codebook supplier, a
      reference creator, a post-hoc checker, an iterative collaborator, an adjudicator, a domain or
      stakeholder validator, or the primary analyst with authority to reject, backtrack, memo, and stop?

   5. RQ5—evaluation: What construct does each automatic or human operation purport to measure;
      is it adequately operationalized, independently validated, compatible with plurality, calibrated,
      source-grounded, and statistically supported; and does workload include verification?

   6. RQ6—validity threats: Could results arise from participant, source, site, diagnosis, platform,
      language, time, boilerplate, interviewer speech, length/position, overlap/leakage, contamination,
      lexical style, same-family judging, anchoring, majority dominance, overconfidence, or omitted
      negative cases?

   7. RQ7—ethics: Are consent, ethics review, privacy, deployment, retention/training terms, bias,
      stakeholder involvement, credential/release security, accountability, and reproducibility
      restrictions reported?

   8. RQ8—opportunity: Which gaps remain after version-family deduplication and full-text inspection
      of the 2025–2026 wave, and which can support a defensible October ARR paper?

3. Search and screening methodology
3.1 Scope, protocol, and eligibility
Eligible primary work presents, evaluates, or substantively analyzes computational support for
qualitative coding or interpretation; proposes evaluation directly applicable to codes or themes; studies
human–AI qualitative analysis; or isolates a validity mechanism that changes such evaluation.
Methodological sources were retained to assess congruence. Pure topic modeling, summarization,
sentiment analysis, or fixed-label prediction without a directly relevant qualitative contribution; studies
that merely use manual TA to study another technology; commercial pages; and unverifiable records
were excluded from the core. Adjacent records were retained when they supplied directly relevant
evidence about constructs, confounding, privacy, or workflow design.

The search began with ten specified seeds and five supplied works and added backward references,
focused forward-title/citation queries, same-author and publication-family searches, and searches for
systems, datasets, and metrics cited by eligible studies. A preprint and proceedings or journal paper
were treated as one family unless their methods materially differed. DeTAILS remains a material
exception: the seven-page CUI paper and its expanded preprint have different evaluative content
(official CUI paper; expanded preprint). Citation chaining was focused and source coverage differed; no
exhaustive citation-saturation claim is made.

3.2 Databases, dates, and identification
All auditable searches were run on 24 August 2026 with a 1 January 2020–24 August 2026 date window
where supported. Within-source deduplication produced 295 PubMed, 688 Crossref, 13 ACL Anthology,
120 OpenReview, and 440 arXiv records. Their union contained 1,556 records; exact DOI and
normalized-title deduplication removed 196, leaving 1,360 unique database records.

4. Conceptual foundations of thematic analysis
“Thematic analysis” is a family, not a single algorithm. Coding-reliability TA usually applies a
comparatively structured frame and can make agreement and adjudication appropriate. Codebook TA
uses a structured coding framework while retaining interpretive judgment. Reflexive TA treats
researcher subjectivity and iterative engagement as analytic resources; themes are constructed patterns
of shared meaning, not labels waiting to be recovered. Braun and Clarke explicitly warn against one-size-
fits-all quality criteria and against importing consensus coding or saturation into reflexive TA as universal
tests (Braun and Clarke, 2021a; Braun and Clarke, 2021b).

Grounded theory requires more than “open” and “axial” labels: constant comparison, memoing,
theoretical sampling, category integration, and theory development are central, with axial coding
belonging to some schools rather than all (Corbin and Strauss, 1990). Qualitative content analysis can be
conventional, directed, or summative, which changes whether categories arise from the data, prior
theory, or counted content plus contextual interpretation (Hsieh and Shannon, 2005). Framework
analysis preserves a case-by-code matrix and is well suited to transparent team comparison ( Gale et al.,
2013). Rapid qualitative analysis uses focused templates and matrices for time-sensitive questions; its
quality depends on purpose, expertise, omissions, and verification, not speed alone (PARRQA, 2024).

These distinctions matter computationally. A model that predicts a short code for each sentence can be
evaluated as labeling. A model that constructs latent themes in RTA cannot be validated solely by
matching one analyst's wording. A grounded-theory system should expose comparisons, memos,
category relations, and sampling decisions rather than merely cluster open codes. A Framework tool
should retain case context and matrix provenance. The common pipeline—segment text, generate
labels, embed or LLM-merge them, compare the output with a consensus set—is usually most congruent
with coding-reliability or codebook TA, even when a paper invokes reflexive language.

This is not a purely terminological objection. McDonald, Schoenebeck, and Forte found substantial
method-label ambiguity in 251 CSCW papers: only 20 of 85 papers reporting open coding described
grounded theory, and fewer than half of the 161 papers coding to identify themes named an analysis
method (McDonald et al., 2019). Reviewer interpretation: computational papers inherit this ambiguity
and then add a measurement error: they often translate “similar to a reference codebook” into “valid
qualitative interpretation.” The appropriate remedy is an explicit analytic contract stating the research
question, unit, tradition, claim level, human role, expected product, and success criteria before running
the model.

The audit also identifies comparatively congruent patterns. Bounded codebook and qualitative-content-
analysis studies can appropriately target code assignment, agreement, or downstream estimates if the
units and labels are independently justified. NITA is congruent with its noncoding, researcher-primary
claim because positionality, counterevidence, theme retention, and the final synthesis remain human

analyst control, although its short, synthetic-corpus study does not establish improved interpretation

models organize researcher-authored summaries in one task and retrieve, verify, and sort quote-
grounded evidence across 167 interviews in another; humans retain contextual interpretation and
intervention decisions (Ronaghi et al., 2026 preprint).

RQ2 answer. The corpus includes reflexive, codebook, and coding-reliability TA; grounded-theory claims;
qualitative content, Framework, and rapid-analysis variants; generic open or deductive coding;
topic/concept discovery; and deliberate noncoding synthesis. Their success criteria are not
interchangeable. Fixed-label F1 or κ supports a bounded assignment claim; cluster coherence supports
organization; reference similarity supports proximity to one reading; none alone establishes reflexive
interpretation, grounded-theory development, or clinically warranted themes. The complete 181-row
extraction preserves each author's label beside the reviewer's operational description so readers can
audit this distinction paper by paper.

5. Taxonomy of computational approaches
5.1 Historical trajectory
Pre-LLM systems used co-occurrence, latent semantic analysis, topic models, embeddings, clustering,
and supervised classifiers to organize text. The conceptual break introduced by instruction-tuned LLMs is
not that clustering disappeared; rather, natural-language generation could draft codes, definitions,
evidence rationales, and hierarchical relations while accepting research questions and codebooks in
ordinary language.

The resulting literature is better represented by ten nonexclusive operational families. A single system
can occupy several families; the list is a task map, not ten mutually exclusive paper bins.

   9. Fixed-label or codebook assignment. Dai et al. co-construct and then apply a codebook; Muse,
      QuaLLM, Turner et al., Than et al., and many applied studies assign defined codes to segments
      (Dai et al., 2023; Matveyenko et al., 2026; Rao et al., 2025; Turner et al., 2026; Than et al., 2025).
      This is the regime in which F1, accuracy, or κ has the clearest target, provided prevalence, units,
      and independent labels are reported.

   10.Open-code suggestion and collaborative code development. Qualitative Code Suggestion,
      CollabCoder, Dai, DeTAILS, LLMCode, MindCoder, and ROSAnnotator expose candidate codes for
      human acceptance, editing, rejection, or comparison (Spinoso et al., 2023; Gao et al., 2024;
      Sharma and Wallace, 2025; Oksanen et al., 2025; Gao et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025). Suggestion
      uptake is not independent validity because seeing a candidate can anchor the analyst.

   11.Hierarchy, schema, axial, and theory-like refinement. HICode, LOGOS, Neo-Grounded Theory,
      and newer open–axial–selective or taxonomy systems merge codes, relate categories, or optimize
      schemas (Zhong et al., 2025; Pi et al., 2025; Wen et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2026). These operations
      implement structure; without theoretical sampling, constant comparison, memo integration, and
      evaluated theory development, they do not establish grounded-theory automation.

   12.Code-first end-to-end theme pipelines. LLM-TA, Auto-TA, SFT-TA, TAMA, and clinical refinement
      systems sequence quotation/code generation, consolidation, themes, evaluation, and report-like
      outputs (Raza et al., 2025; Yi et al., 2025, Auto-TA preprint/poster; Yi et al., 2025, SFT-TA
      preprint/non-archival poster; Xu et al., 2026; Yi et al., 2026). An end-to-end artifact chain is not
      evidence of end-to-end interpretive validity.

   13.Noncoding narrative and theme development. NITA explicitly makes coding optional and keeps
      positionality, counterevidence, theme retention, and the meta-narrative with researchers




                                                                                                       Page 14
SYSTEMATIC SCOPING REVIEW | ACL ARR 2026



      (Nguyen, Trung, and Nguyen, 2026). This family matters conceptually because “theme generation”
      need not be implemented as clustering increasingly abstract labels.

   14.Interactive workbenches and reflexivity support. LLooM, DeTAILS, Muse, CentaurTA, Co-Refine,
      Reflexis, and Teleoscope provide editable concepts, persistent feedback, drift/disagreement
      prompts, or branchable analytic graphs. Reflexis shows that deliberation and positionality
      interfaces exist; the open question is whether they improve independently judged interpretation
      over time.

   15.Autonomous, role-prompted, and deliberative pipelines. Thematic-LM, TA-MAS, Auto-TA, TAMA,
      and related systems assign coder, aggregator, reviewer, or critic roles. “Multi-agent” can mean
      one model repeated under role prompts, same-model deliberation, or genuinely different model
      families. Borchers et al. found only 5 of 432 reported contrasts favoring multi-agent consensus
      and 65 favoring single agents, so consensus cannot be assumed to provide independent
      triangulation (Borchers et al., 2026).

   16.Adaptation, fine-tuning, and steering. Parfenova et al. use LoRA for short-code generation;
      Ganesh et al. show task-specific fine-tuning can outperform in-context learning; SFT-TA trains on
      paraphrases derived from ten of twelve reference themes; and the AIED confidence study tests
      activation steering (Parfenova et al., 2025; Ganesh et al., 2024; Yi et al., 2025; Li et al., AIED 2026

The expanded DeTAILS preprint reports high suggestion-to-revision F1. That can indicate useful
alignment, but without a no-suggestion arm it cannot distinguish assistance from researchers retaining
the machine's framing (Sharma et al., 2025 expanded preprint). CentaurTA makes feedback persistent,

disagreement focus and 12/12 felt they retained control, while its two author-raters judged all seven
triggered drift alerts and 18 prompts relevant; the lack of a baseline, synthetic corpus, and
nonindependent ratings limits outcome claims (Ye et al., 2026).

The K–12 instrument pipeline provides a procedural counterexample to token oversight. Researchers
read raw data beside model output, memoed misleading or generic proposals, retained every
merge/category decision, and then three trained coders applied the resulting 67-item hierarchy to a
message- and conversation-disjoint 2,560-message sample and added five missed codes (Liu et al.,

three-message windows. These denominators describe different analytic stages and must not be
interchanged.

Parfenova et al.'s human study gives a compact example of metric–construct divergence. BERTScore
favors closeness to the reference, yet three researchers evaluating fifteen items sometimes preferred
human alternatives with greater analytic depth; Krippendorff's alpha was only .20 (Parfenova et al.,
2025). HICode uses many-to-many matching at several cosine thresholds, a better topology for
legitimate splits and merges than forced one-to-one matching, but two original annotators produced
human precision/recall different from cosine results and agreed with alpha .31 (Zhong et al., 2025).
Dörfel and Ammoneit's full-text comparison makes the divergence especially concrete: 13 of 14 LLM-
derived codes cleared a .60 semantic-similarity threshold against human codes and repeated
applications reached mean Jaccard .83, yet global coding-unit overlap with the human analysis was only
.39 and the LLM code system lost domain-specific distinctions (Dörfel and Ammoneit, 2026). These are
not reasons to abandon semantic matching; they are reasons to validate it on the intended interpretive
task and at the unit where the analytic claim is made.

Reviewer interpretation among the inspected full texts: Chen et al. provide the most developed no-
single-gold diagnostic in this evidence map. Four human and four differently prompted Gemma-3-27B
coders generate codebooks for 127 community messages; an aggregate code space supports Coverage,
Overlap, Novelty, and Jensen–Shannon Divergence, with ten runs used for stability. The authors
explicitly frame the measures as a profile rather than a universal score. Their simulations also reveal
failure: code flooding can improve Coverage, Overlap, and Novelty, while concepts omitted by every
coder remain outside the aggregate space (Chen et al., 2026). The method replaces one gold codebook
with a team-relative universe, an important but incomplete step toward plurality.

Among the inspected full texts, the July 2026 Agreement Is Not Quality preprint supplies a substantial

Bradley–Terry model interleaves humans and models. This directly demonstrates that agreement and
blind expert preference can diverge in both directions (Liu et al., 2026). Its boundary is equally
important: one verifier, one codebook, one platform, a selected model set, and code assignment rather

8.2 Human evaluation
Human protocols vary from one expert inspecting one transcript to blinded multi-analyst comparisons.
TAMA's expert is clinically qualified but helped create the original reference themes; five lay reviewers

metric with human scores on only ten items and do not report the number of experts (Parfenova and

8.3 Cost and workload
Runtime is routinely compared with human coding hours, but verification is often excluded. QuaLLM
reports 145.49 million tokens and a cost of $1,662.30 (Rao et al., 2025). TAMA reports machine
processing in minutes versus roughly 30 hours per human coder on AAOCA (Xu et al., 2026). Turner et al.
estimate 97% time and 99% cost reductions for deductive dementia-trial coding, but 42.6% of 929
extracted excerpts were incorrect, making uncounted adjudication material (Turner et al., 2026).
Ronaghi et al. report 30% and 55% time reductions for two researchers on the site-summary task and
scale quote-grounded analysis to 167 interviews, while also documenting retrieval fragmentation,
contextual correction, and continuing human synthesis (Ronaghi et al., 2026). Vikan et al.'s rigorous
offline reflexive use found no time saving and documented fabricated quotations and changed meanings
(Vikan et al., 2025). Efficiency claims should include cleaning, segmentation, prompt/codebook
development, retries, source verification, false-positive review, privacy review, documentation, and
reruns after model drift.

RQ5 answer. Evaluation ranges from exact/fuzzy matching, F1/accuracy, κ/alpha, lexical and embedding
similarity, one-to-one or many-to-many matching, coverage/novelty/coherence, repeated-run stability,
and LLM judging to blinded expert comparison, quotation audits, participant/source coverage,
downstream estimates, workload, and cost. No single operation validates all qualitative claims.
Adequacy depends on the declared construct, independent evaluators, plurality assumptions, source-
level error categories, representative sampling, uncertainty and statistical units, calibration, and the
verification work required to make outputs safe.

9. Validity, reliability, and interpretability
9.1 A reference interpretation is not objective ground truth
Human themes are evidence, but their epistemic role depends on the method. In coding-reliability
designs, agreement with a carefully developed codebook can be the target. In reflexive TA, a reference
is one situated reading. Several studies acknowledge this yet still organize quantitative evaluation
around a consensus set. TAMA calls its twelve themes reliable ground truth while later noting that
thematic interpretations are not unique (Xu et al., 2026). Dai's LLM participates in constructing the final
codebook, so subsequent agreement is partly a compatibility test rather than independent discovery
(Dai et al., 2023). A valid plural design should preserve alternative codes, rationales, and disagreements
rather than collapse them before evaluation.

9.2 Reliability is conditional, not validity
Repeated-run similarity, inter-rater agreement, and consistent code assignment are valuable. They
answer whether a procedure behaves similarly under specified conditions. They do not show that the
stable output is warranted. Auto-TA's bidirectional ROUGE across ten runs can identify lexical instability,
but cannot establish qualitative dependability (Yi et al., 2025). Bijker et al. obtain substantial agreement
across multiple ChatGPT conversations in content analysis, yet that alone cannot reveal shared bias or
systematic misreading (Bijker et al., 2024). Hill et al.'s 93% agreement paired with κ .34 illustrates
prevalence sensitivity, while Gwet AC1 remained about .92–.93 (Hill et al., 2026). Reporting only the
most favorable reliability coefficient invites misinterpretation.

Confidence is a separate construct and cannot repair this problem by assertion. In the AIED coding
study, mean verbal confidence near .9 accompanies accuracy of .338–.520 and ECE of .403–.553; a .95
anchor is copied in 96.5% of outputs and leaves discrimination near chance (AUC .518) (Li et al.,
2026/2027). Rationale similarity can prioritize disagreements for review, but Tajik et al.'s large rationale-
similarity difference (d=1.16) is a triage signal, not evidence that either agent is correct (Tajik et al.,
2026).

9.3 Abstraction is not automatically quality
Systems often reward concise, nonredundant, semantically coherent themes. But high abstraction can
erase cases, and embedding homogeneity can favor broad topics over interpretive themes that connect
tension. In Zhu et al., BERTopic reduced thousands of labels to fewer than sixty, increasing apparent
relevance but reducing formal-human-code capture and producing some tenuous, stereotypical, or
unsupported labels (Zhu et al., 2026). In LLM-TA, a rare cardiac arrest can become disproportionately
salient (Raza et al., 2025). Conversely, topic models of autistic/ADHD youth interviews underrepresented
or missed masking, self-doubt, and strengths, and higher-Jaccard iterative analysis still missed low-


                                                                                                        Page 32
SYSTEMATIC SCOPING REVIEW | ACL ARR 2026



frequency concerns (Lukito et al., 2026; Logan et al., 2026). Theme count, hierarchical depth, parsimony,
and coherence should therefore be treated as design properties, not quality outcomes.

9.4 Interpretability requires warrants
A theme name and several relevant quotations are inspectable, but an interpretive warrant must explain
why those passages support that theme, how contrary passages were handled, and whose accounts are
represented. A same-family judge can check surface consistency without revealing shared assumptions.
TAMA's one-transcript GPT-4o-mini audit found no hallucinated themes and high grounding for GPT-4o
output, but the authors acknowledge circularity and the limited audit (Xu et al., 2026). The 2026 trauma
study is more revealing because source-linked examination differentiates likely valid novel codes from
tenuous “reading between the lines” and unsupported stereotypes (Zhu et al., 2026).

10. Provenance, auditability, and reproducibility
Provenance has evolved from optional quotations to structured lineage. Official paper records and their
linked artifacts document resources for Dai (paper; code), LLooM (paper; code), HICode (paper; code),
DeTAILS (paper; code), CentaurTA (paper; code), TAMA (paper; code), LLM-TA (preprint; code), Muse
(paper; evaluation data), and the trauma pipeline (paper; code). Reviewer judgment among the
inspected full texts: no single implementation dominates every provenance layer. The 2026 clinical
refinement preprint carries stable turn IDs through chunks, quotations, normalized codes, typed
relations, subthemes, themes, and LLM-reviewer revisions (Yi et al., 2026); Reflexis retains immutable
code histories and disagreement/positionality events (Ye et al., 2026); Teleoscope provides branchable
graph histories and analyst comparison (Lee et al., 2026); and ROCK specifies interoperable source
spans, codebooks, relations, and versions (Peters et al., 2026).

Logging is not validation. The provenance paper uses GPT-4o-mini in generation and evaluation roles
and reports no independent human validation of the ledger's interpretive warrants; public code,
complete prompts, and generated ledgers were not located in the inspected version (Yi et al., 2026).
Auto-TA has quote IDs but maps the fraction of used quotes to “credibility/confirmability,” which
rewards reuse without checking representativeness (Yi et al., 2025). Reflexis validates prompt relevance
and perceived control, not whether its histories improve evidential sufficiency; Teleoscope reports
analyst confidence in saturation, not held-out coverage; ROCK is a specification rather than an
adoption/outcome study. A theme can be perfectly traceable to one participant and still misrepresent
the population.

A provenance-aware evaluation should test five layers separately: (1) exactness—does the cited string
occur and retain speaker/context? (2) entailment/warrant—does it support the stated code/theme? (3)
coverage—which relevant passages and participants are omitted? (4) distribution—is evidence
concentrated in a few speakers/sites? and (5) audit usability—can an independent analyst reproduce
the path, inspect versions, and register disagreement? Stratified human audits should sample high/low-
frequency themes, negative cases, early/middle/late context positions, and every participant stratum.

Computational reproducibility remains limited by proprietary drift. Exact dated model identifiers,
temperature, top-p, prompt text, context/chunking, retry policy, seeds or run IDs, tool versions, human
edits, and stopping rules are often incomplete. Kempny et al. found that 45% of 75 studies did not
specify deployment configuration, 75% reported no parameter settings, 61% supplied at least partial
prompts, and 13% supplied no prompting details (Kempny et al., 2026). Restricted qualitative data can
be ethically necessary, but papers can still release synthetic fixtures, schemas, prompt/configuration
files, hashes, transformation logs, and a runnable audit harness.

11. Confounding, identity leakage, and shortcut learning
The expanded corpus contains direct confound and positionality evidence, so a claim that such tests are
absent is no longer defensible. Lukito et al. find that apparent diagnosis clustering in autistic/ADHD
interview topics is confounded by interviewer identity and is not robust after adjustment (Lukito et al.,
2026). Subbiah et al. cross race/gender conditions, three open models, and five seeds on 154 life
narratives and observe demographic-conditioned semantic, affective, and thematic shifts, stereotyping,
and consequential omissions (Subbiah et al., 2026). Ashwin et al. regress coding errors on respondent
attributes: GPT-3.5 errors are associated at the 5% level with characteristics for 10 of 19 codes, versus 1
of 19 for a bespoke classifier, and downstream regression estimates can be distorted (Ashwin et al.,
2025/2026). Yu, Liu, and He find much higher within-company than cross-company similarity in
earnings-call representations and strong alignment with a ticker-level historical baseline, showing how
“text signal” can be repeated-source identity (Yu et al., 2025). These results demonstrate specific failure
mechanisms; they do not prove shortcut use in every theme generator.

Several pathways are visible. Mathis et al. merge interviewer and interviewee speech, making facilitator
language inseparable from respondent content (Mathis et al., 2024). Hill et al. explicitly exclude
facilitator segments yet report that facilitator material drives much of the comprehensive error when
systems mishandle source boundaries (Hill et al., 2026). Disease-background prompts in clinical studies
can turn diagnosis language into a shortcut. Random chunk splits can leak the same participant or
interviewer across train and test. Elangovan, He, and Verspoor show the general effect of input overlap:
in SST-2, accuracy rises from 85.0 in the least-overlapping stratum to 96.8 in the most-overlapping
stratum (Elangovan et al., 2021).

Adjacent task studies sharpen the mechanism. Rizvi et al. show that keyword and lexical cues are
insufficient for nuanced ableism classification; Sakib et al. directly probe spurious correlations in social-
determinants extraction; and Wei et al.'s China/Japan/Korea platform comparison entangles platform,
language, geography, time, and preprocessing and may remove small minority clusters (Rizvi et al.,
2025; Sakib et al., 2025; Wei et al., 2026). These are not interchangeable with inductive TA, but they
justify subgroup, platform, source, and lexical-shortcut checks in a qualitative pipeline.

Long-context and order effects create a second confound. Evidence near the middle of long contexts can
be used less reliably than evidence near the beginning or end (Liu et al., 2024). Few-shot exemplar order
can change performance from near state of the art to near random, with good orders failing to transfer
(Lu et al., 2022). A fixed temperature of zero does not control model drift, hidden serving
nondeterminism, chunk order, exemplar order, or context position.

Contamination is an unresolved ambiguity when public corpora and published human themes are used
with closed models. Deng et al.'s TS-Guessing method found that ChatGPT and GPT-4 exactly recovered


                                                                                                      Page 35
SYSTEMATIC SCOPING REVIEW | ACL ARR 2026



a missing MMLU option in 52% and 57% of cases, demonstrating that contamination can be probed
rather than merely asserted (Deng et al., 2024). The AAOCA reference themes, source papers, and
disease description are public. Reviewer interpretation: this creates a memorization risk, not evidence
that the systems memorized them. A qualitative benchmark should document availability dates, use
fresh/unpublished references where possible, mask disease/site/paper names, test paraphrased labels,
insert secret placebos/canaries, and attempt missing-theme guessing before exposing the corpus.

The appropriate confound suite combines participant/interviewer/site/domain-disjoint folds; duplicate
and nearest-neighbor analysis; probes for participant/source/site identity in embeddings; ablations of
facilitator turns, boilerplate, demographics, disease names, and metadata; counterfactual identity
swaps;    chunk    and     codebook-order     permutations;   evidence-position   randomization;     and
rare/contradictory-case insertion. Report theme quality alongside source-probe accuracy and
participant-level residual error. A model that remains accurate only within source should be described
as source-conditioned, not generally interpretive.

RQ6 answer. Demonstrated or credible threats include participant/interviewer/source identity,
demographic conditioning, platform/language/time entanglement, overlapping participants or chunks,
codebook/reference leakage, development-set reuse, prevalence and class imbalance, lexical style
sensitivity, public-corpus contamination, long-context position and order effects, same-family judging,
human anchoring, stable shared bias, majority dominance, and omission or sensational amplification of
rare cases. Causal shortcut claims require controlled ablation or counterfactual evidence; otherwise the
report labels them risks rather than facts.

12. Ethics and high-stakes clinical use
Ethical reporting is uneven even when papers discuss ethics in principle. Kempny et al. found that 95%
of their broad sample mentioned ethical considerations and 97% incorporated some human verification,
but these aggregate categories do not show whether consent covered vendor processing, whether
retention was disabled, or who is accountable for an unsupported clinical theme (Kempny et al., 2026).

Local inference offers a concrete privacy path. Mathis et al. run a quantized LLaMA-2-70B model on local
dual RTX 3090 hardware for protected mental-health interviews (Mathis et al., 2024). Vikan et al. use
offline Mistral-7B, report ethics references and consent that explicitly covers LLM use, and retain human
authorship/accountability (Vikan et al., 2025). Grover et al. use local Hermes-3-Llama-3.1-70B on five
Canadian focus groups and keep data access under research-ethics control (Grover et al., 2026). Zhu et
al. run local open models, withhold survivor transcripts, and manually check that released codes are not
identifying (Zhu et al., 2026).

De-identification is not authorization and does not eliminate risk. Baroud et al. annotate 6,199 indirect
identifiers across 100 MIMIC-III notes, showing how contextual combinations remain sensitive (Baroud
et al., 2025). Morris et al. reconstruct 92% of 32-token inputs exactly from embeddings and recover 89%
of full names from MIMIC-note embeddings, so hosted embedding APIs and vector stores require raw-
text-level governance (Morris et al., 2023). A defensible paper should state the ethics determination,
consent and secondary-use scope, whether LLM processing was disclosed, explicit and indirect de-
identification, deployment and processor/subprocessor, region, encryption, retention, training use,
deletion verification, access control, and incident response.

The expanded evidence also shows why provider assurances and reproducibility artifacts require
inspection. Mehta et al. send translated, sensitive focus-group material through an API/Colab workflow;
the paper's assurance about provider access is broader than what it documents (Mehta et al., 2025).
Frontier privacy policies are fragmented and change over time, and King et al.'s prose generalization
about default training is broader than its own table supports (King et al., 2025). The repository linked to
Wei et al. prints an API key and contains language/label inconsistencies; public code therefore improves
auditability only when secrets, configurations, and paper–code consistency are checked (official paper;
repository). Gustavsen et al. exclude a participant at reidentification risk while explicitly noting that this
protection can silence a marginalized voice (official paper).

Representational harm is not hypothetical. In firearm-violence interviews, safety refusals are triggered

13. Critical synthesis
13.1 Points of agreement
Across the inspected architectures, clear definitions, inclusion/exclusion criteria, relevant examples, and
bounded tasks generally improve code application. Muse's error analysis attributes much disagreement
to under-specified codebooks and inconsistent human annotation; its best ensemble reaches κ .703 only
on well-specified codes and after per-code tuning (Matveyenko et al., 2026). Dai's iterative rationales
and examples produce high human–machine agreement on small survey tasks (Dai et al., 2023). HICode
and LLooM show that iterative abstraction scales better than one monolithic prompt (Zhong et al., 2025;
Lam et al., 2024). Evidence links and analyst-editable artifacts make outputs more inspectable and
usable in these systems, even though they do not prove validity.

Ronaghi et al. sharpen the bounded-assistance conclusion in applied health services. Their model can
organize researcher summaries and retrieve, verify, and sort quotation-grounded evidence across 167
interviews, making a deadline-constrained synthesis feasible; it cannot supply the actionable nuance,
novelty judgment, contextual correction, or final intervention decisions, which remain human work
(Ronaghi et al., 2026). NITA reaches a similar governance conclusion through a noncoding design, and
Reflexis operationalizes optional disagreement and positionality support without replacing analyst
authority (NITA; Reflexis).

The inspected evidence also repeatedly finds that output quality is conditional on prompt wording, role,
chunking, model, threshold, codebook definition, context, and run. Zhu et al. run 118 experiments and
find large quality changes from small processing/prompt differences; model size changes relevance only
modestly (Zhu et al., 2026). Muse finds eight of fifteen models outperform GPT-4o under its chosen
prompt but recognizes that GPT-oriented optimization may favor nearby models (Matveyenko et al.,
2026). TAMA's local Llama-3.1-8B evaluator approves poor themes early, while GPT-4o requires expert
stopping because an automatic score plateaus and over-refinement appears (Xu et al., 2026).

13.2 Points of conflict
Reviewer synthesis among the inspected full texts: the sharpest conflict is between high
semantic/reference alignment and qualitative adequacy. Short-label studies report respectable
BERTScore, but human experts prefer deeper alternatives and disagree substantially (Parfenova et al.,
2025). Dörfel and Ammoneit likewise obtain 13/14 clear semantic code mappings and mean run stability
of Jaccard .83, while coding-unit overlap with the human analysis is only .39 and domain-specific
distinctions are lost (Dörfel and Ammoneit, 2026). Clinical systems recover most reference themes,
while audits find culturally specific omissions, dramatic rare-case overrepresentation, wrong source
attribution, or narrative erasure (Sakaguchi et al., 2025; Raza et al., 2025; Hill et al., 2026; Zhu et al.,



                                                                                                     Page 40

2026). These are not contradictory facts: a system can match the central, majority reference concepts
while disagreeing at the applied unit or mishandling the edges where qualitative insight and ethical
stakes are greatest.

A second conflict concerns automation. Thematic-LM and Auto-TA frame autonomous end-to-end
generation as scalable; DeTAILS, Dai, LLooM, TAMA, Muse, and CentaurTA retain varying degrees of
analyst control (Qiao et al., 2025; Yi et al., 2025; DeTAILS; Dai et al., 2023; Lam et al., 2024; Xu et al.,
2026; Matveyenko et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026). The empirical evidence favors automation of
bounded operations and human authority over interpretation. Role prompts labeled “qualitative
researcher,” “surgeon,” or “layperson” do not create epistemic standpoint or accountability.

13.3 What apparent gaps are already partly closed
   • No-gold evaluation: partly addressed by Chen's aggregate code space and Liu et al.'s blind,
      source-symmetric verification. Shared omissions, flooding, one-verifier dependence, theme-level
      warrants, and cross-domain construct validation remain (Chen et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2026).

   • Provenance: architecturally addressed by quote IDs, clinical ledgers, MindCoder, ROSAnnotator,
      Reflexis histories, Teleoscope branches, and ROCK's versioned standard. Exactness, sufficiency,
      omission, participant spread, and independent-auditor usability are not jointly validated.

   • Human feedback and reflexivity: addressed by Dai, DeTAILS, TAMA, Muse, CentaurTA, Co-Refine,
      NITA, Nyaaba, Reflexis, and the K–12 instrument pipeline. Open questions concern anchoring,
      longitudinal outcome change, undetected error, burden, and whether deliberation improves
      independent interpretation rather than satisfaction.

   • Multi-agent analysis: already crowded; novelty cannot rest on adding roles. Borchers' 5/432
      versus 65/432 contrast directions make independent benefit especially doubtful. Agent identity,
      interaction, and advantage over strong sequential calls need ablation (Borchers et al., 2026).

   • Fine-tuning and adaptation: demonstrated by Parfenova et al., Ganesh et al., SFT-TA, and other
      LoRA/steering studies; held-out concept/domain/model-family generalization and leakage-safe
      adaptation remain open.

   • Grounded-theory and noncoding workflows: schema/hierarchy systems, LOGOS, Neo-Grounded
      Theory, and open–axial–selective instruments exist; NITA demonstrates a deliberate noncoding
      alternative. Full theoretical sampling and validated theory development remain unestablished
      among inspected systems.

   • Stability and stopping: repeated runs, no-new-theme rules, saturation ratios, fixed rounds,
      refinement trajectories, iterative-versus-batch comparisons, drift alerts, and analyst saturation



                                                                                                      Page 41
SYSTEMATIC SCOPING REVIEW | ACL ARR 2026



       confidence all exist (Aggregate Code Space; De Paoli–Mathis; QualiGPT; Borchers et al.; Logan;
       Reflexis; Teleoscope). Crossed seed×prompt×order×sample×model×analyst uncertainty and a
       held-out stopping rule tied to supported minority/negative-case gain and verification burden
       remain open.

   • Confidence/abstention: rationale and confidence routes exist, but severe miscalibration, anchor
       copying, and weak steering make an uncalibrated confidence threshold unsafe (Li et al.,
       2026/2027).

   • Multilingual and culturally situated work: Finnish, Italian, German, Dutch, Japanese, translated
       East African, and East Asian platform studies exist (Kousa et al.; Sakaguchi et al.; Mehta et al.;
       Ashwin et al.). Breadth, native-speaker evaluation, and cross-site/model transfer remain limited
       and uneven.

15. Recommended ARR research direction
The ranking is reviewer judgment, not a result of the quality-score totals. It weighs novelty after the
inspected literature, ACL relevance, ethical importance, and seven-week feasibility. All three projects
require a frozen protocol, exact model/configuration records, repeated runs, an explicit analytic
contract, and source-grounded human evaluation.

Rank 1 — VoiceTrace: participant- and source-aware validity for LLM thematic analysis
Provisional title. Whose Voice Survives? Participant, Source, and Negative-Case Audits for LLM-Assisted
Thematic Analysis

Central claim. Conventional theme similarity can remain high while a pipeline relies on source/identity
cues or loses, misattributes, stereotypes, and overamplifies participant evidence. A joint provenance-
and-perturbation audit reveals these failures and changes system rankings or substantive conclusions.

Research questions. (1) How much code/theme variation is explained by participant, interviewer, site,
identity   condition,   and    context     position?   (2)   Which   participant-coverage,   concentration,
counterevidence, and source-warrant measures expose failures hidden by semantic overlap and LLM
judging? (3) Which chunking, masking, retrieval, model-family, abstention, and human-review choices
preserve rare and contradictory accounts?

Novelty relative to inspected work. Provenance, demographic sensitivity, and reflexivity are each
occupied. Subbiah directly demonstrates demographic-conditioned narrative shifts; Lukito shows
interviewer confounding; Parkington, Bang, Mehta, and Gustavsen expose omitted or altered evidence;
MindCoder, ROSAnnotator, clinical ledgers, Nyaaba, Reflexis, Teleoscope, and ROCK provide lineage or
deliberation components; NITA preserves researcher authority. The defensible novelty is the joint
controlled validation of exact source lineage, participant/source perturbations, voice and negative-case
preservation, and independent warrants—not a new ledger or dashboard.

Datasets required. Two multi-speaker corpora with stable participant/source IDs and authorization for
model processing, ideally one approved clinical or health-services corpus and one public nonclinical
corpus; one site/domain fully held out. If fresh clinical authorization is not already complete, use an
approved existing corpus plus a public set and a human-checked synthetic perturbation suite. Do not use
AAOCA as the sole test family.

Experimental design. Freeze prompts before held-out evaluation. Cross at least one strong proprietary
and one open-weight model with chunking, source masking, evidence retrieval, and three independent
runs. Use participant/interviewer/site/domain-disjoint partitions; source-identity probes; removal of
facilitator, boilerplate, diagnosis, and site cues; counterfactual identity swaps; rare/negative-case



                                                                                                    Page 50
SYSTEMATIC SCOPING REVIEW | ACL ARR 2026



insert/remove/prevalence tests; context-position moves; and no-source-ID versus exact-source-ledger
ablations. Report exact evidence precision, attribution error, participant recall/coverage/concentration,
negative-case survival, unsupported-claim rate, conventional one/many-to-many similarity with
threshold sensitivity, and at least one downstream conclusion change.

Human evaluation required. Separate reference creators from evaluators. Use at least two qualitative-
method experts and two domain experts, blinded to condition and source, with independent judgments
before adjudication. Stratify by participant prevalence, disagreement, negative-case status, source
position, and model confidence. Ask whether the cited evidence supports the claim, what relevant
evidence is omitted, and whether counterevidence changes the interpretation. Add purposive
participant/stakeholder review for high-stakes claims if permitted.

Ethics requirements. Confirm consent and secondary-use scope for model processing; use local or
approved enterprise deployment; document retention/training terms and embedding risk; suppress
identifying quotations; preregister subgroup analyses; involve community/domain advisers in sentinel
construction; and name the human who owns and can stop the interpretation.

Key baselines. Human-only analysis; single-pass/no-lineage generation; a codebook assignment
baseline; HICode-style hierarchy; Thematic-LM/Auto-TA-style role pipeline; ROCK-compatible exact-
source ledger; Reflexis-style deliberation; NITA-style researcher-primary condition; Subbiah-style
demographic conditioning; no-source-ID ablation; and calibrated versus uncalibrated review routing.

Expected contribution. A benchmark and protocol, participant/source-aware metrics, controlled causal
probes, and evidence that common rankings or conclusions change when voice preservation is
measured. Major risk: unavailable multi-site data or insufficient expert time. Mitigation: freeze a two-
corpus minimum, preregister the smallest defensible expert sample, release the perturbation/audit
harness, and scope clinical claims to authorized data. October feasibility: high only if data and experts
are secured immediately; otherwise medium with a nonclinical main experiment.

Rank 2 — WarrantBench: plural, source-symmetric evaluation of codes and themes
Provisional title. Beyond Agreement: Evaluating Competing Qualitative Interpretations Through
Evidence Warrants

Central claim. A multi-expert, source-grounded protocol that preserves alternative readings,
counterevidence, and participant coverage can identify which automatic measures are useful under
different analytic contracts without assuming one correct interpretation.

Research questions. (1) How do lexical, embedding, matching, plural-code-space, ICR, LLM-judge, blind-
preference, provenance, and coverage measures relate to expert judgments at code and theme levels?



                                                                                                  Page 51
SYSTEMATIC SCOPING REVIEW | ACL ARR 2026



(2) Do relationships change under coding-reliability, codebook, and reflexive contracts? (3) Which metric
portfolio transfers to an unseen domain and model family while detecting unsupported or
unrepresentative interpretations?

Novelty relative to inspected work. Aggregate Code Space, source-symmetric Bradley–Terry
comparison, IDEAlign perturbations, Simon's blinded comparison, specialist/lay clinical ratings, ICR,
Han's LLM-judge calibration, Misra's context/theme-fit audit, and Logan's iterative/batch comparison
already cover parts of the problem. Novelty must be theme-level, multi-expert, cross-contract, source-
grounded construct validation on held-out domains/models, not another scalar metric or “agreement
is not quality” slogan.

Datasets required. Three domains with source IDs and at least two genuinely independent human
interpretations per domain; a minimum viable version can use two deep domains plus a held-out
transfer set. Include different qualitative contracts and purposively sampled minority/negative cases.

Experimental design. Generate outputs from two model families and two workflows. Build balanced
code/theme comparisons covering paraphrase, split/merge, contradiction, generic abstraction,
unsupported novelty, shared omission, source misattribution, and minority evidence. Experts blind to
source judge central claim, evidence sufficiency, counterevidence, participant spread, alternative
defensibility, and contract fitness. Fit hierarchical/generalizability models with evaluator/domain effects;
hold out one domain and one model family; publish calibration, intervals, and error strata rather than
one leaderboard.

Human evaluation required. At least three qualified experts per domain or a crossed incomplete-block
design supporting reliability and generalizability estimates. Collect rationales and positionality
information, retain individual disagreement before adjudication, randomize order, and disclose sampling
and multiplicity.

Ethics requirements. Fair compensation; consent-compatible source quotation use; de-identification
and access control; no unnecessary source-identity exposure; respectful disagreement-preserving
release; and stakeholder input on harms and rubric language in sensitive domains.

Key baselines. ROUGE/METEOR; BERTScore/cosine; one-to-one and many-to-many matching; Aggregate
Code Space dimensions; IDEAlign; ICR; repeated-run similarity; same- and cross-family LLM judges;
source-symmetric Bradley–Terry; human-oracle/reference creators; quote exactness and participant
coverage. No LLM judge may be the sole verifier.

Expected contribution. An empirical construct map showing which metrics predict independently
warranted interpretation under which contract, plus a reusable judgment set. Major risk: annotation
burden and the objection that judgments remain situated. Mitigation: preregister constructs, use a


                                                                                                     Page 52
SYSTEMATIC SCOPING REVIEW | ACL ARR 2026



powered incomplete-block design, preserve rationales/disagreement, and test generalization rather
than claiming a universal gold metric. October feasibility: medium; two rigorous domains are preferable
to three shallow ones.

Rank 3 — StopTA: held-out evidence-based stopping for iterative refinement
Provisional title. When Should an Agent Stop Refining? Evidence-Gain Criteria for Iterative Thematic
Analysis

Central claim. Fixed rounds, no-new-theme rules, similarity plateaus, confidence, and judge scores do
not reliably identify improvement. A frozen rule balancing marginal supported participant/negative-case
evidence against redundancy, unsupported claims, instability, and verification burden transfers better
across models and domains.

Research questions. (1) What changes over refinement trajectories at evidence, participant, theme, and
conclusion levels? (2) Which stopping rule transfers across models, domains, and pipelines? (3) Does
exposing a stopping signal help analysts avoid over-refinement without anchoring or hiding minority
cases?

Novelty relative to inspected work. CAILA implements no-new-theme stopping; De Paoli–Mathis
estimate saturation; TALLMesh allocates transcript batches; LOGOS and LLMCode retain refinement
trajectories; TAMA shows plateau and expert stopping; clinical refinement runs up to ten rounds;
CentaurTA can degrade; Teleoscope captures analyst saturation confidence; Logan directly compares
iterative and batch modes. The open claim is transferable held-out stopping with a joint evidential
objective, not invention of a stopping signal.

Datasets required. Three existing public or approved corpora with stable source IDs, including at least
one with rare/negative cases and one held-out domain. Save every intermediate artifact and human
revision.

Experimental design. Produce complete ten-round trajectories for at least two models and two
pipelines. Learn or calibrate only on development corpora, freeze the rule, and test on the third.
Evaluate marginal supported evidence, participant/negative-case coverage, redundancy, unsupported
claims, substantive instability, and verification time. Report Pareto fronts and component ablations;
never select the best round on test material.

Human evaluation required. Blinded experts compare adjacent iterations and stopped outputs for
supported improvement, lost nuance, counterevidence, minority preservation, and verification burden.
A small analyst study can test whether a signal changes stopping behavior and whether it anchors users.




                                                                                                 Page 53
SYSTEMATIC SCOPING REVIEW | ACL ARR 2026



Ethics requirements. Apply the same source/privacy controls as Rank 1; recognize that intermediate
artifacts can retain sensitive quotations; minimize retention; and make the analyst—not the stopping
score—the final authority.

Key baselines. Fixed 1/3/5/10 rounds; no-new-theme; saturation ratio; TALLMesh allocation; iterative
versus batch; same- and cross-family judge thresholds; reference-similarity plateau; human stop;
Teleoscope-style saturation confidence; confidence/abstention; and Pareto-front reporting.

Expected contribution. Public stopping trajectories, a frozen rule, and evidence that “more refinement”
is not monotonic and that the chosen stopping point transfers. Major risk: a hand-weighted composite
that overfits. Mitigation: preregister components, fit no weights on test data, compare Pareto
alternatives, and validate with independent human trajectories.              October feasibility: high
computationally and medium for the required expert study.

Recommendation
Choose Rank 1 if participant/source identifiers, processing authorization, and evaluator access are
already secured. Reviewer judgment among the inspected full texts: it offers the strongest combination
of novelty, ACL relevance, and ethical consequence because it joins mechanisms that existing studies
examine separately. Choose Rank 3 if those prerequisites are unavailable within days; it is more feasible
but must demonstrate held-out transfer. Rank 2 is potentially the most foundational, but the existing
no-gold and judge-calibration literature makes a rushed single-domain or single-expert version unlikely
to clear the novelty bar.

16. Limitations of the review
This is a single-reviewer, Codex-assisted scoping review, not an independently duplicated screen or
extraction. All 1,360 deduplicated database records now have terminal title/abstract dispositions, but
terminal does not mean error-free: the initial 122/612/626 high/uncertain/low triage was only a
prioritization device, and the later eligibility decisions were not independently adjudicated. Parallel
extraction, schema assertions, DOI/title deduplication, evidence locations, and record-specific reasons
improve auditability but do not create inter-reviewer agreement.

Retrieval was incomplete. The primary unique-identity flow sought 317 reports, did not retrieve 53, and
assessed or resolved 264; the latter produced 237 retained reports, 18 full-text exclusions, and nine
publication/version-family resolutions. No methodological or result claim in this synthesis derives from a
nonretrieved report or publisher abstract. The appendix gives every nonretrieval its recorded retrieval
disposition and every full-text exclusion its record-specific reason; four nonretrieval source records lack
a specific reason and are labeled only as not retrieved after documented attempts rather than being
supplemented by inference. Because plausible full texts remain inaccessible, all novelty and absence
claims are bounded to reviewer judgment among the inspected full texts.

Coverage also limits recall. PubMed and arXiv supplied reproducible exports, but Crossref searches were
relevance-ranked and capped, the arXiv identity query was capped, ACL retrieval was title-oriented, and
OpenReview candidates were client-filtered. Stable auditable bulk exports were unavailable for Google
Scholar, Semantic Scholar, ACM DL, IEEE Xplore, Web of Science, and Scopus in the available
environment. Citation chaining preserved parent-seed provenance but was focused and index-
dependent; no exhaustive saturation claim is made.

Publication status near the cutoff is unusually fluid. Preprint, online-first, acceptance, event, and formal-
proceedings years can differ. Auto-TA is arXiv:2506.23998v2 with OpenReview label ACL-SRW 2025
Poster; no archival ACL Anthology paper was verified (preprint; OpenReview history). SFT-TA is
arXiv:2509.17167v1 with OpenReview label ML4H 2025

(preprint; OpenReview history). The retained AIED confidence paper was available as a 2026 conference
manuscript but has a formal Springer 2027 proceedings citation and is labeled accordingly (Li et al.). The
ATA-LLM paper was associated with the SEET 2025 event and published in 2026 (Beltrán et al.). Where
peer review or acceptance was not established by 24 August 2026, the bibliography says so rather than
inferring status from a DOI or template.

The corpus is heterogeneous and dependence-rich, so no meta-analysis was attempted. Identically
named metrics use different formulas; coding units and denominators differ; studies reuse datasets,

17. Conclusion
LLMs now assist nearly every visible artifact of qualitative work: familiarization proxies, quotation
retrieval, open and deductive codes, codebooks, hierarchies, noncoding themes, memos and schemas,
reports, disagreement/confidence triage, stopping signals, evidence links, version histories, and
standards. Reviewer synthesis among the inspected full texts: the most defensible systems keep these
artifacts editable and traceable, preserve analyst authority and disagreement, declare the analytic
contract, calibrate uncertainty, and audit sources and participants with independent human judgment.

The evidence is strongest for well-specified code application, retrieval, and scalable organization. It is
weaker—not wholly absent—for reflexive, culturally situated, minority-sensitive, theory-building, and
clinically consequential interpretation. New direct evidence makes the boundary clearer: high semantic
similarity, run agreement, topic coherence, or verbal confidence can coexist with low coding-unit
overlap, quote alteration, systematic overcoding, rare/implicit-theme loss, demographic distortion, and
interviewer confounding. A source link improves auditability but does not prove that an interpretation is
exact, sufficient, representative, or warranted.

RQ8 answer. The open research opportunity is no longer “generate themes with agents,” “add
provenance,” “prompt reflexivity,” “repeat runs,” or “stop when no new themes appear.” Those
components exist. The strongest defensible ARR direction is a joint participant/source-aware validity
audit that controls identity and context while measuring exact evidence, omission, concentration,
negative-case survival, independent warrants, and changed conclusions. Multi-domain multi-expert
warrant validation and held-out evidence-based stopping remain credible alternatives; calibrated
abstention is an important component, not a substitute for either. These are reviewer judgments
bounded to 24 August 2026, the inspected full texts, and immediate feasibility constraints.
