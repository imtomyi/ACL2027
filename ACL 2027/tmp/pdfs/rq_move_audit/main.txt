357   7   Limitations
358   The inferential scope is limited to the recruited re-
359   viewers, frozen models and prompts, two English
360   or English-translated corpora, and five flaw fam-
361   ilies. Role prompting cannot reproduce human
362   training, lived experience, accountability, or inter-
363   pretive judgment. Controlled variants isolate single
364   defects, whereas natural outputs may contain sub-
365   tler or interacting errors, and routing may create
366   false reassurance for items that are not escalated.
367   Finally, this task assesses bounded evidence pack-
368   ets, not complete qualitative analysis, which also
369   requires familiarization, memoing, reflexivity, and
370   accountable interpretation.

371   8   Ethics Statement
372   Real-text model processing and rater exposure be-
373   gin only after institutional, source, provider, ac-
374   cess, retention, and release requirements are doc-
375   umented; approval of the original collection does
376   not transfer to this study. Restricted copies are
377   minimized, protected text goes only to approved
378   services, and every excerpt shown to raters or re-
379   leased receives a two-person privacy and permis-
380   sions review. Raters participate voluntarily, and
381   public artifacts contain only aggregate outputs and
382   specifically cleared material. Details appear in Ap-
383   pendix B.

384   9   Conclusion
385   WarrantRoute tests one central claim: source-
386   grounded flaws may benefit from different reviewer
387   roles. RQ1 evaluates human expertise-aware rout-
388   ing; RQ2 evaluates frozen LLM reviewer proxies
389   on the same bounded task and permits a human
390   comparison only after RQ1 is complete. The cur-
391   rent manuscript establishes the protocol and reports
392   no experimental result. [AFTER THE FROZEN
