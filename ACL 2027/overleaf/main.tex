\begin{abstract}
Large language models can generate candidate qualitative coding outputs, but different source-grounded flaws may require different reviewers. We present \textbf{WarrantRoute}, an expertise-aware design that routes a theme-and-evidence packet to qualitative-methods review, domain review, both, or neither. RQ1 asks whether expertise-aware routing improves error detection. RQ2 asks how human and LLM reviewers compare on the same items and roles. RQ3 asks whether the routed human review from RQ1 actually improves the output. We test whether its feedback helps a fixed revision process repair a verified error without creating a new one. The paired human and LLM comparison requires completed RQ1 data and a range fixed in advance for deciding when performance is similar. Dreaddit and GoEmotions support development and in-domain audits, while CaChe and ParlaMint-GB provide held-out cross-domain evaluation. \end{abstract}
